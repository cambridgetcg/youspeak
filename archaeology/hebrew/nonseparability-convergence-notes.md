# Non-separability convergence — excavation notes

_Companion notes, 2026-11-10, to the dig at
`archaeology/hebrew/yetzer-hara-yoma-69b.md`.
These notes do excavation only: they coin and score nothing. Every citation carries a status
tag: **[verified: …]** means opened in this session at the stated locus; **[from memory: not
re-checked]** means exactly that._

The region in question is the one the declined forge 327-symphyme restated in its verdict as
**non-separability without cost**: a good and its shadow grown so far together that removing
one costs the other (labs/logos/experiments/core/327-symphyme.md:231).

---

## 0. Three shapes that are easily conflated

| | Shape | What it claims | Test: does anything ever come apart? | Test: can one bad branch be pruned while the root stays? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| (i) | **Mixed now, separable later** | Two things of distinct origin are entangled for the present. Separating them now damages the good; a later time or a better sorter can separate them cleanly. | Yes, later. | Not now (collateral damage); fully, later. |
| (ii) | **One root bearing two fruits** | One source, drive or process produces both a good and a harm. Cutting the root costs both. The fruits are distinct effects. | The fruits are distinct; the root never comes apart from its fruits. | Often yes, at the edges; the root cannot be removed without the good. |
| (iii) | **One thing under two descriptions** | There are not two things. The same item is called good under one description (effect, reading, realisation) and bad under another. | Never: there is nothing to separate. | No. Identity forbids even edge-pruning; changing one description changes the item. |

A fourth pattern turns up and should not be forced into the three:
**(iv) reversal over time**, where each state carries the latent seed of its opposite
(Daodejing 58). It is closest to (ii) set out in time.

**Which shape is Yoma 69b? (ii), one root bearing two fruits.** The drive (in Rashi's gloss,
the generative heat of living things) yields procreation and transgression. Imprisoning the
root stops the good ("no day-old egg in all the land of Israel"). A clean split of the fruits
by Heaven is refused ("a half, in Heaven they do not give"). One bad branch (arousal toward
close kin) is pruned by blinding, and Rashi says the drive still works toward other forbidden
unions. That last fact rules out (iii), because an identity reading would forbid pruning one
object while others remain. It also rules out (i), because the good and the bad never come
apart and nobody sorts them later. [verified: Sefaria, Yoma 69b seg. 12; Rashi on Yoma 69b and
on Sanhedrin 64a; details in the dig §3]

Yoma also runs a control. In the same story the **idolatry** inclination is captured and
sealed in a lead pot with no recorded cost to the world; the only price named is the
petitioners' renunciation of the reward for resisting it (seg. 6). The tradition therefore
tells apart an evil that can be removed and one that cannot. Any gap sentence built on this
witness has to carry that discrimination, not a blanket claim. [verified: Sefaria, Yoma 69b
seg. 6, 9–10]

## 1. Witnesses

For each: form, IPA, gloss, source, shape, how close it is to Yoma's shape and to the region,
and whether it is independent of the others.

### 1.1 Rabbinic (anchor) — b. Yoma 69b; b. Sanhedrin 64a; Genesis Rabbah 9:7

See the dig. Summary: *yetzer ha-ra* יֵצֶר הָרָע /ˈjetseʁ haˈʁa/; Genesis Rabbah 9:7, "'very good' —
this is the evil inclination … were it not for the evil inclination, a man would never build a
house, would never marry a wife, would never beget children, and would never engage in
commerce" [verified: https://www.sefaria.org/api/texts/Bereshit_Rabbah.9.7]. Shape (ii),
stated in the midrash as an identity (iii) and defended as (ii). Language family: Northwest
Semitic (Hebrew and Jewish Babylonian Aramaic).

### 1.2 The wheat and the tares — Matthew 13:24–30, explained at 13:37–43

- **Forms.** ζιζάνια *zizania* "tares, darnel" (sg. ζιζάνιον); σῖτος *sitos* "wheat";
  συναυξάνεσθαι *synauxanesthai* "to grow together"; ἐκριζώσητε *ekrizōsēte* "you would uproot".
- **IPA.** ζιζάνιον: 5th c. BCE Attic /zdiz.dá.ni.on/; 1st c. CE /ziˈza.ni.on/ (Wiktionary labels this
  row "1st CE Egyptian"; its 4th CE Koine row is the same) [verified:
  https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/ζιζάνιον]. συναυξάνεσθαι, approximate Koine
  /sy.na(f)kˈsa.nes.θe/ [from memory: not re-checked; note that Wiktionary's 1st c. CE scheme still has
  aspirated /tʰ/ for θ, so /θ/ fits a later Koine]. σῖτος, Attic /sîː.tos/, 1st c. CE /ˈsi.tos/
  [verified: https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/σῖτος, by the verifier].
- **Text** (SBLGNT) [verified: https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Matthew%2013%3A24-30&version=SBLGNT
  and …13%3A36-43…]:
  - 13:25 *ἦλθεν αὐτοῦ ὁ ἐχθρὸς καὶ ἐπέσπειρεν ζιζάνια ἀνὰ μέσον τοῦ σίτου* — "his enemy came and sowed tares among the wheat".
  - 13:29 *Οὔ, μήποτε συλλέγοντες τὰ ζιζάνια ἐκριζώσητε ἅμα αὐτοῖς τὸν σῖτον* — "No, lest in gathering the tares you uproot the wheat along with them".
  - 13:30 *ἄφετε συναυξάνεσθαι ἀμφότερα μέχρι τοῦ θερισμοῦ* — "let both grow together until the harvest"; then *Συλλέξατε πρῶτον τὰ ζιζάνια … τὸν δὲ σῖτον συναγάγετε* — "gather the tares first … but the wheat gather".
  - 13:38–39: the tares *εἰσιν οἱ υἱοὶ τοῦ πονηροῦ* "are the sons of the evil one"; *ὁ δὲ ἐχθρὸς ὁ σπείρας αὐτά ἐστιν ὁ διάβολος* "the enemy who sowed them is the devil"; *ὁ δὲ θερισμὸς συντέλεια αἰῶνός ἐστιν* "the harvest is the end of the age".
- **Gloss of the plant.** "darnel, ryegrass (*Lolium temulentum*)" [verified: Wiktionary, a
  tertiary source; LSJ could not be opened — lsj.gr and Perseus refused automated access].
- **Shape: (i), mixed now and separable later.** The non-separability is temporary and
  practical. The two plants have different origins (one sown by an enemy), and a clean
  separation comes at the harvest.
- **How close.** Close only on the cost clause: uprooting the bad now would uproot the good
  with it (13:29). Far on everything else. The bad is **foreign** (sown in by an enemy), which
  puts this witness next to **kumarme** (power from outside) and against Yoma (a native drive).
  And separation is promised, which is the opposite of the region.
- **Independence and contact.** **Not independent of the Rabbinic milieu.** It belongs to a
  first-century Jewish setting. Wiktionary derives ζιζάνιον through Aramaic (Syriac
  *zīzānā*, "ryegrass"), ultimately from Sumerian *zizān* "wheat", and lists a Mishnaic Hebrew
  זזנין among the cognates [verified as Wiktionary's claim only; not corroborated by a primary
  lexicon]. The Mishnah pairs wheat with *zunin* (הַחִטִּים וְהַזּוּנִין אֵינָן כִּלְאַיִם זֶה בָזֶה,
  "wheat and *zunin* are not mixed kinds with each other", m. Kilayim 1:1) [verified:
  https://www.sefaria.org/api/texts/Mishnah_Kilayim.1.1]. Jastrow glosses *zunin* "[degenerate
  wheat,] a weed growing among wheat, darnel or rye-grass" and derives it from Hebrew זָנָה, not
  from the Greek or Syriac word [verified: Jastrow s.v. זוֹנִין I, https://www.sefaria.org/api/words/זונין,
  by the verifier]. So the two traditions share a field and a plant; that they share the
  plant-word is **not** shown (the earlier "probably a plant-word" is withdrawn). They should
  still not be counted as separate witnesses to a shape.

### 1.3 Greek φάρμακον *pharmakon*

- **Form and IPA.** φάρμακον, Attic /pʰár.ma.kon/ [verified: https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/φάρμακον].
- **Senses** (Wiktionary, tertiary; LSJ blocked): "drug (whether healing or noxious)";
  "healing drug; medicine; remedy"; "potion; charm; spell"; "poison"; "dye; coloring".
  Etymology unclear, possibly Pre-Greek (Beekes, per Wiktionary); Mycenaean *pa-ma-ko*
  [verified: Wiktionary only].
- **Plato, *Phaedrus* 274e–275a** (Burnet's Greek, Perseus canonical-greekLit TEI)
  [verified: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/PerseusDL/canonical-greekLit/master/data/tlg0059/tlg012/tlg0059.tlg012.perseus-grc2.xml]:
  - Theuth, 274e: *μνήμης τε γὰρ καὶ σοφίας φάρμακον ηὑρέθη* — "for it has been found a pharmakon of memory and of wisdom".
  - Thamus, 275a: *τοῦτο γὰρ τῶν μαθόντων λήθην μὲν ἐν ψυχαῖς παρέξει μνήμης ἀμελετησίᾳ … οὔκουν μνήμης ἀλλὰ ὑπομνήσεως φάρμακον ηὗρες* — "for this will produce forgetfulness in the souls of those who learn it, through neglect of memory … so you have found a pharmakon not of memory but of reminding".
  - Thamus' judgement frames it: *ἄλλος δὲ κρῖναι τίνʼ ἔχει μοῖραν βλάβης τε καὶ ὠφελίας* — "another [is able] to judge what share of harm and benefit it has" (274e).
- **Homer, *Odyssey* 4.230** [verified, same repository, tlg0012.tlg002, book 4 line n="230"]:
  *φάρμακα, πολλὰ μὲν ἐσθλὰ μεμιγμένα πολλὰ δὲ λυγρά* — Egypt bears "pharmaka, many good when
  mixed, many baneful". The next line makes every Egyptian a physician "knowing beyond all
  men" (*ἰητρὸς δὲ ἕκαστος ἐπιστάμενος περὶ πάντων ἀνθρώπων*).
- **Shapes.** *Phaedrus*: (iii) in part. One thing (writing) is a remedy for reminding and the
  cause of forgetting, described by its two effects. Thamus does not call it a pharmakon "that
  destroys memory"; he denies it is a pharmakon of memory at all and says it will produce
  forgetfulness. *Odyssey* 4.230: (i), a land holding many drugs, good and baneful, with every
  inhabitant a knowing physician (that the physicians "tell them apart" is my gloss on
  *ἐπιστάμενος*, not the text's wording). The word itself: one substance whose effect depends on dose or
  use, which is the threshold structure 327:54 already noted.
- **How close.** Moderate. No removal test is run. The harm follows from use and dose, and
  nothing says that removing the poison removes the cure.
- **Independence.** Greek, independent in origin of the Semitic witness. Its Derridean
  afterlife (327:52) is a reading, not another witness.

### 1.4 The churning of the ocean — Bhāgavata Purāṇa 8.7; Mahābhārata Ādiparvan 1.15–17

- **Forms and IPA** [IPA from memory: not re-checked]: *hālahala* हालहल /ɦaːlɐɦɐlɐ/ (the
  poison, BhP 8.7.18; *hālāhala* /ɦaːlaːɦɐlɐ/ at 8.7.42); *kālakūṭa* कालकूट /kaːlɐkuːʈɐ/ (the poison's name in the Mahābhārata vulgate);
  *amṛta* अमृत /ɐmr̩tɐ/ ("deathless", the nectar); *nīlakaṇṭha* नीलकण्ठ /niːlɐkɐɳʈʰɐ/
  ("blue-throated", Śiva); *Dhanvantari* धन्वन्तरि /dʱɐnʋɐntɐri/.
- **BhP 8.7.18** [verified: https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/8/7/18/, transliteration and
  word-glosses]: *nirmathyamānād udadher abhūd viṣaṁ / maholbaṇaṁ hālahalāhvam agrataḥ* —
  "from the ocean being churned there arose **first** (*agrataḥ*) a very fierce poison named
  hālahala". The page spells it *hālahala*, not *hālāhala*, but the same text spells it
  *hālāhalaṁ* at 8.7.42, so nothing should rest on the vowel length. **Read on** (by the
  verifier): 8.7.42 *tataḥ karatalī-kṛtya vyāpi hālāhalaṁ viṣam / abhakṣayan mahā-devaḥ* — Śiva
  takes the poison in his palm and drinks it; 8.7.43 *yac cakāra gale nīlaṁ* — it leaves his
  throat blue; 8.8.31–35 — Dhanvantari rises from the churning and the demons snatch his jar of
  nectar (*kalasaṁ cāmṛtābhṛtam*, 8.8.35) [verified: https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/8/7/42/,
  …/8/7/43/, …/8/8/31/, …/8/8/35/].
- **Mahābhārata, Critical Edition 1.16** [verified: TITUS machine-readable text based on the
  Poona Critical Edition (Tokunaga), files mbh016.htm and mbh017.htm at
  http://titus.uni-frankfurt.de/texte/etcs/ind/aind/mbh/, ids M_1_16_1–40]: the churning gives
  in order Soma the moon (16.33), Śrī, Surā and a white horse (16.34), the Kaustubha gem
  (16.35), and Dhanvantari *śvetaṃ kamaṇḍaluṃ bibhrad amṛtaṃ yatra tiṣṭhati*, "bearing the
  white water-pot in which the amṛta stands" (16.37), followed by the Mohinī episode
  (16.39–40). **Neither 1.16 nor 1.17 contains any word for the poison** (a search for *viṣa*
  other than *viṣṇu*, *kālakūṭa*, *hālahala*, *hālāhala*, *garala*, *nīlakaṇṭha* found
  nothing; the only *kaṇṭʰa* in 1.17 is Rāhu's throat, reached by the amṛta). 1.15 (13 verses,
  mbh015.htm) was opened by the verifier: it sets up the churning (*matʰyamāne mahodadʰau*,
  15.12) and has no poison word either. TITUS credits the text as "prepared on the basis of the
  critical edition by Sukthankar, Belvalkar e.a. … Poona 1937–1964; by Muneo Tokunaga"
  [verified: http://titus.uni-frankfurt.de/texte/etcs/ind/aind/mbh/mbh001.htm, mbh015.htm, by the
  verifier].
- **Mahābhārata vulgate, Ganguli's translation, Adi Parva §XVIII** [verified:
  https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/the-mahabharata-mohan/d/doc3881.html]: after
  Dhanvantari "with the white vessel of nectar" and the elephant Airāvata, "But with the
  churning still going on, the poison Kalakuta appeared **at last**. … And then Siva, being
  solicited by Brahman, swallowed that poison for the safety of the creation. The divine
  Mahesvara held it in his throat, and it is said that from that time he is called Nilakantha
  (blue-throated)." [The Nīlakaṇṭha sentence was re-opened on the page by the verifier and is
  there.] That Ganguli translates the vulgate rather than the Critical Edition is [from memory:
  not re-checked].
- **Correction to a common summary.** "The poison halāhala arises before the amṛta" is true of the
  **Bhāgavata Purāṇa** (8.7.18, *agrataḥ*). It is **not** true of the Mahābhārata: the
  constituted Critical Edition text has no poison at all in the churning, and the vulgate puts
  the poison (named *kālakūṭa*) **after** the amṛta. That the Rāmāyaṇa's Bālakāṇḍa puts
  *hālāhala* first is [from memory: not re-checked].
- **Shape: (ii) with separation achieved.** One source (the ocean, one churning) yields both
  the poison and the nectar. They come out at different times and are separated, at a cost
  borne by a **third party** (Śiva takes the poison so creation survives).
- **How close.** Close to the "one root, two fruits" half of Yoma; far from non-separability,
  because the story separates them. Its distinctive lesson is that the cost of separation is
  carried by a bearer. In the Bhāgavata order the poison has to be dealt with before the nectar
  is reached.
- **Independence.** Indian (Indo-Aryan), independent of the Semitic and Greek witnesses. The
  Bhāgavata is later than the epic and its order is not the epic's [dating from memory: not re-checked].

### 1.5 煩惱即菩提 — "afflictions are awakening" (with 性惡, "evil in the nature")

- **Form and IPA.** 煩惱即菩提 *fánnǎo jí pútí*, Mandarin /fan˧˥ nau̯˨˩˦ tɕi˧˥ pʰu˧˥ tʰi˧˥/;
  Japanese *bonnō soku bodai* /bonnoː soku bodai/; Sanskrit *kleśa* /kleːɕɐ/ and *bodhi*
  /boːd̪ʱi/ [IPA from memory: not re-checked; the verifier lengthened the *o*, which is always long
  in Sanskrit].
- **Textual home** (CBETA full-text and keyword-in-context APIs; 572 occurrences in 386
  juan-level results; by author, most in Zhiyi, 52) [verified: https://cbdata.dila.edu.tw/stable/search?q=煩惱即菩提
  and …/search/kwic?work=…&juan=…]. Dates below are CBETA's catalogue dates (`time_from`–`time_to`)
  [verified: https://cbdata.dila.edu.tw/stable/works?work=…, by the verifier].
  - **Earliest dated layer in CBETA: Sui authors, with the phrase already quoted as scripture.**
    Jingying Huiyuan (淨影慧遠, 523–592), 大乘義章 T1851, juan 18, p. 828b09: 故經說言「凡夫未成佛，菩提為煩惱。聖若成佛時，煩惱即菩提。」
    — "therefore a sūtra says: 'While an ordinary being has not become a buddha, awakening is
    affliction; when the sage becomes a buddha, affliction is awakening.'" The same quotation is
    in his 維摩義記 T1776, juan 3, p. 484b16. Which sūtra he means was not identified.
  - **Indian treatises, in translations later than Zhiyi.** 大乘莊嚴經論
    (*Mahāyānasūtrālaṃkāra*, T1604, tr. Prabhākaramitra 波羅頗蜜多羅, Tang, 630–633), juan 6,
    p. 622b13: 偈曰：由離法性外，無別有諸法，是故如是說，煩惱即菩提。釋曰：如經中說，無明與菩提同一。
    — "Verse: because apart from dharma-nature there are no separate dharmas, therefore it is
    said: affliction is awakening. Commentary: as a sūtra says, ignorance and awakening are one."
    Also 攝大乘論釋論 (T1596, tr. Dharmagupta 笈多 with 行矩 and others, Sui, 605–616), juan 10,
    p. 316a14: 煩惱即菩提，生死寂滅體. **Both translations postdate Zhiyi (d. 597), so neither
    can be the Chinese source of his phrase.** They show that the Indian treatises carry a
    corresponding idea; the Sanskrit of these verses was not opened. (The earlier version of
    these notes called T1604 the "earliest layer"; that was wrong.)
  - **Zhiyi (538–597, CBETA dates), Tiantai.** 摩訶止觀 T1911, juan 8, p. 116b24 (欲令眾生於魔界即佛界、於煩惱即菩提) and juan 9,
    p. 131a26 (又無明即法性，煩惱即菩提); 四教義 T1929, juan 11, p. 761b20; 四念處 T1918, juan 4,
    p. 574a10, which gives the reason and a citation: 煩惱性不障菩提、菩提不障煩惱，煩惱即菩提、菩提即煩惱，故《淨名》云「佛為增上慢人說斷婬怒癡，名為解脫。無增上慢者，婬怒癡性即是解脫。」
    — "the nature of affliction does not obstruct awakening … hence the Vimalakīrti says: for
    the arrogant the Buddha teaches that cutting off lust, anger and delusion is liberation;
    for those without arrogance, the nature of lust, anger and delusion is itself liberation."
    (The Vimalakīrti's own wording was not opened.)
  - **Chan.** 六祖大師法寶壇經 (*Platform Sūtra*, Zongbao edition, T2008, compiled 1290 per
    CBETA), juan 1, p. 350b28:
    凡夫即佛，煩惱即菩提。前念迷即凡夫，後念悟即佛。前念著境即煩惱，後念離境即菩提。 — "ordinary
    beings are buddhas; afflictions are awakening. The previous thought deluded is an ordinary
    being; the next thought awakened is a buddha. The previous thought attached to objects is
    affliction; the next thought detached from objects is awakening." This line is from the late
    Zongbao text. The Dunhuang text (T2007) does not return 煩惱即菩提 in CBETA; it has
    煩惱即是菩提 once, at p. 342b16, in a different setting (the exchange with Zhicheng)
    [verified: CBETA KWIC, T2007 juan 1, by the verifier].
  - **性惡, the strongest statement of non-excisability in any witness.** 觀音玄義 T1726
    (CBETA byline 智顗說．灌頂記), juan 1, p. 882c11: 問：闡提與佛斷，何等善惡？答：闡提斷修善盡，但性善在；佛斷修惡盡，但性惡在。問：性德善惡何不可斷？答：性之善惡但是善惡之法門，性不可改，歷三世無誰能毀，復不可斷壞。
    — "Q: The icchantika and the Buddha each cut off what good and evil? A: The icchantika has
    cut off cultivated good entirely, yet nature-good remains; the Buddha has cut off
    cultivated evil entirely, yet nature-evil remains. Q: Why cannot the good and evil of the
    nature be cut off? A: the good and evil of the nature are simply the dharma-gates of good and
    evil; the nature cannot be altered; through the three times none can destroy it, nor can it
    be cut off." **Read to the end of the exchange** (by the verifier, 882c16 ff.): asked whether
    the Buddha's uncut nature-evil lets cultivated evil arise again, the text answers no. The
    Buddha "penetrates evil, and because he penetrates evil he is free with respect to evil
    (於惡自在), so he is not stained by evil and cultivated evil cannot arise … because he is free,
    he broadly uses the dharma-gates of evil to transform beings (廣用諸惡法門化度眾生), using them
    all day and all day unstained." So the retained nature-evil is not a cost; it is the means of
    the Buddha's work among the deluded [verified: CBETA KWIC, T1726 juan 1, q=佛不斷性惡]. That the
    work's attribution to Zhiyi is disputed in scholarship is [from memory: not re-checked]; the
    CBETA byline is 智顗說．灌頂記.
- **Shape: (iii), one thing under two descriptions** (same dharma-nature, the same mind deluded
  or awakened). 性惡 adds a distinction that parallels Yoma's control case: **cultivated evil
  (修惡) is removable entirely; evil in the nature (性惡) is not.**
- **How close.** On non-excisability, closer than anything else. On cost, far: there is no
  removal experiment and no price, because identity makes separation meaningless. The Platform
  Sūtra's version is moment to moment (the same mind, two thoughts), not a standing organ.
  Unlike Yoma, it forbids even edge-pruning of the nature, while it allows total removal of the
  acted-out evil.
- **Independence.** Indian Mahāyāna (Yogācāra, Vimalakīrti) → Chinese Tiantai and Chan is **one
  lineage**, independent of the Semitic, Greek and Diné witnesses. The phrase is not a Tiantai
  coinage: Huiyuan, outside Tiantai and older than Zhiyi, already quotes it as a sūtra line.
  The 327 file's "Zhiyi's 即 propositions" is right that Zhiyi used it, more than anyone else in
  CBETA, but should not imply that he originated it.

### 1.6 Daodejing 58 — 禍兮福之所倚

- **Text** (Wang Bi edition, Chinese Wikisource) [verified: https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/道德經_(王弼本)]:
  其政悶悶，其民淳淳；其政察察，其民缺缺。禍兮福之所倚，福兮禍之所伏。孰知其極？其無正。正復為奇，善復為妖。人之迷，其日固久。是以聖人方而不割，廉而不劌，直而不肆，光而不燿。
- **Forms and IPA** (Mandarin) [from memory: not re-checked]: 禍 *huò* /xu̯ɔ˥˩/ "misfortune";
  福 *fú* /fu˧˥/ "fortune"; 倚 *yǐ* /i˨˩˦/ "lean on"; 伏 *fú* /fu˧˥/ "lie hidden"; 割 *gē*
  /kɤ˥/ "cut".
- **Gloss** (my rendering): "Misfortune: that on which fortune leans. Fortune: that in which
  misfortune lies hidden. Who knows where it ends? There is no fixed norm. The upright turns
  again into the strange; the good turns again into the ominous. … Therefore the sage is square
  but does not cut, sharp-edged but does not wound."
- **Shape: (iv), reversal and latency over time**, closest to (ii) set in time. There is no
  standing organ; states turn into one another. *方而不割* "square but does not cut" is a
  practical maxim next to the tares' "do not uproot", not a claim of non-separability.
- **How close.** Moderate-low. No removal test, no constitution.
- **Independence.** Chinese, independent of the others. Variant texts (Mawangdui, Guodian)
  were not checked.

### 1.7 Augustine and Aquinas — evil parasitic on good

- **Augustine, *Enchiridion*** (NPNF trans., New Advent) [verified: https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/1302.htm]:
  ch. 11 "For what is that which we call evil but the absence of good?"; ch. 13 heading "There
  Can Be No Evil Where There is No Good; And an Evil Man is an Evil Good"; ch. 14 "evil cannot
  exist without good, or in anything that is not good" and "From what is good, then, evils
  arose, and except in what is good they do not exist". **Read on** (by the verifier): ch. 14
  continues "Good, however, can exist without evil", which states the asymmetry outright; and
  ch. 11 opens "even that which is called evil, when it is regulated and put in its own place,
  only enhances our admiration of the good", with God able to "bring good even out of evil" — an
  instrumental use of evil, in the region's direction, that these notes had left out. Ch. 10
  calls the created whole "very good", which echoes Genesis 1:31, the verse Genesis Rabbah 9:7
  expounds (the echo is my reading; the NPNF text gives no reference). The Latin (for example
  *privatio boni*) was not opened [from memory: not re-checked]; *privatio boni*
  /priːˈwaː.ti.oː ˈbo.niː/ [IPA from memory].
- **Aquinas, *Summa theologiae* I q.48** (English Dominican trans., New Advent) [verified:
  https://www.newadvent.org/summa/1048.htm]: a.2 ad 3, "many good things would be taken away if
  God permitted no evil to exist; for fire would not be generated if air was not corrupted, nor
  would the life of a lion be preserved unless the ass were killed". The reply opens by citing
  Augustine, *Enchir.* 11 ("God is so powerful that He can even make good out of evil"), and
  ends: "Neither would avenging justice nor the patience of a sufferer be praised if there were
  no injustice." a.3: "evil imports the absence of good. But not every absence of good is evil."
- **Shapes.** Augustine: (iii) with an **asymmetry**: one subject is good by nature and evil by
  privation, and evil depends on good but not the reverse. Removing the good removes the evil,
  which is the **opposite direction** to the region (where removing the shadow costs the good).
  Aquinas a.2 ad 3: a separate shape, (ii′) **the good of one bearer is the corruption of
  another** (lion and ass, fire and air). This is ecological interdependence across bearers,
  and it is the removal test argued at the scale of the cosmos ("many goods would be taken
  away"), like Zechariah's warning.
- **How close.** Aquinas ad 3 is close in argument (take away the evils and goods go with
  them), far in structure (two bearers, not one drive). Augustine is apt for the asymmetry and
  wrong for the direction, except for ch. 11's instrumental clause (evil "put in its own place"
  serves the good), which is the seed of Aquinas' argument. Even there, "Good, however, can exist
  without evil" (ch. 14) denies the region's central claim.
- **Independence.** **One lineage** (Augustine → Aquinas; Aquinas' reply cites the *Enchiridion*
  directly; the Neoplatonic background is [from memory: not re-checked]). Count it as one witness.
  It is also not wholly independent of the anchor: it shares the Hebrew Bible's "very good" with
  Genesis Rabbah 9:7, and it shares a tradition with Matthew 13.

### 1.8 Outside Eurasia — Diné (Navajo): Monster Slayer spares Old Age, Cold, Poverty, Hunger

A real witness exists and was opened. It is the closest structural parallel to Yoma found.

- **Source.** Washington Matthews, *Navaho Legends* (1897), "The Navaho Origin Legend", III "The
  War Gods", ¶¶360–367 and n. 157 [verified: Project Gutenberg #60165,
  https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/60165/pg60165.txt]. Matthews' spellings: *Nayénezgani*
  ("Slayer of the Alien Gods"), *anáye* (alien gods), *San* (Old Age), *Hakáz Estsán* (Cold
  Woman), *Tieín* (Poverty), *Ditsi'n* (Hunger), *Estsánatlehi* (Changing Woman), *Ni'ltsi* (Wind).
- **Modern orthography and IPA** [from memory: not re-checked]: *Naayééʼ Neizghání*; *naayééʼ*
  /naːjéːʔ/; *Sá* /sá/ "old age"; *dichin* /tìtʃʰìn/ "hunger"; *hakʼaz* "cold". I am not
  confident of the modern spelling of Matthews' *Tieín* and do not give one.
- **What the text says.** For Old Age, Cold Woman and Poverty, the Wind whispers that the being
  still lives and Changing Woman will not say where; for Old Age she stays silent although he asks
  four times (¶360; again ¶362, ¶364). For Hunger only the Wind's directions are recorded (¶366).
  Monster Slayer finds each and announces that he has come to kill it (raising his weapon only
  at Cold Woman, ¶363), and each pleads:
  - Old Age (¶361): "if you kill me there will be no increase of men; the boys will not grow up to become fathers; the worthless old men will not die; the people will stand still. It is well that people should grow old and pass away and give their places to the young. Let me live, and I shall help you to increase the people." Spared.
  - Cold Woman (¶363): "if you kill me it will always be hot, the land will dry up, the springs will cease to flow, the people will perish." Spared.
  - Poverty (¶365): "it would not be well for the people … then they would always wear the same clothes and never get anything new. If we live, the clothing will wear out and the people will make new and beautiful garments". Spared.
  - Hunger (¶366): "If we die, the people will not care for food; they will never know the pleasure of cooking and eating nice things, and they will never care for the pleasures of the chase." Spared.
  - ¶367: "every one I meet now speaks to me as a relation". Note 157 records a Version A in which the hero says he has found "none that were not my friends".
- **Shape: (ii), one root bearing two fruits, run as a removal test** (the hero can kill; the
  harm pleads the cost; the hero spares). Old Age argues from **increase of men**, the
  generative argument that Yoma makes through the missing eggs.
- **How close.** The closest in structure. Differences: each harm is a separate being rather
  than one drive with many objects; they are spared whole, with no partial measure like the
  blinding; and nothing is refused from above. The cost is stated by the harm itself, not
  observed.
- **Independence.** **Independent** (Na-Dene, North America; no plausible contact with rabbinic
  literature). Caveats: Matthews' English shapes the wording, and he records variant versions;
  whether earlier Spanish and missionary contact before his recording (published 1897) left
  any trace on this episode was not examined (the verifier's caveat; no evidence either way was
  opened).
- **Protocol.** A living, sacred tradition. My understanding is that origin narratives are told
  in their proper season and setting [from memory: not re-checked]. Any use beyond scholarly
  citation needs consultation with Diné knowledge-keepers and scholars.
- **Contradiction with the repo.** archaeology/navajo/hozho-cosmology-foundations.md:300–307
  says the beings were "preserved by Changing Woman's intervention", lists Old Age, Cold,
  Poverty and Lice (no Hunger), gives the Diné term for Cold as *Dibáá'*, and gives moral
  rationales ("don't learn endurance / gratitude", "become complacent"). Matthews' version has
  Changing Woman withholding where they live (a silence that could be read as protective, but it
  is not an intervention that saves them: each is spared by Monster Slayer on its own plea),
  Hunger rather than Lice, Cold Woman as *Hakáz Estsán*, and generative or ecological rationales. Other versions may include lice; the file should say
  which version it follows. That *dibáá'* means "thirst" rather than "cold" is [from memory:
  not re-checked].

## 3. Notes on re-gapping (what the evidence will carry)

- The evidence carries a **three-part** claim: removing the root costs the good; a clean split
  is unavailable; pruning at the edge is possible, cheap and small. It does not carry "any
  reduction of the exposure costs the capacity in the same measure" (327's original gap), nor
  "only by maiming, and the maiming is the price" (327's refined verdict), because the blinding
  records no cost to the good.
- A gap sentence on this witness has to say **which** shadows the claim covers. Yoma's control
  case (idolatry sealed away with no recorded cost to the world; the petitioners had renounced
  only the reward for resisting it) and Tiantai's 修惡 / 性惡 distinction (acted-out evil cut
  entirely, nature-evil not) both separate a removable evil from a non-removable one.
- On orientation (327's HARMONE objection 1): the Rabbinic and Diné witnesses both run
  harm-first ("this harm is necessary"). Tiantai runs both ways (煩惱即菩提 and 菩提即煩惱).
  None of the witnesses runs strictly good-pole-first ("your empathy is your handle"). A forge
  that wants the good-pole-first orientation is choosing it, not receiving it from the donors.

## 5. Contradictions with the repo found while digging

1. **327-symphyme.md:212** ("the eggs return"), **:229** ("The eggs come back") and **:178** ("with procreation preserved"). The Gemara says none of these; they are inferences from the release (Yoma) and from Rashi. [verified against Sefaria Yoma 69b and Sanhedrin 64a]
2. **327-symphyme.md:212, :214, :218, :220, :227, :235** quote "They gouged out its eyes". That is the Davidson translation. The Aramaic *כַּחְלִינְהוּ* is "they blinded (lit. painted with kohl)" (Jastrow s.v. כְּחַל I sense 2; Rashi: "blinded its eyes by means of kohl"). [verified]
3. **327-symphyme.md:178, :181, :193, :202, :212, :218, :227** quote "Heaven does not grant half gifts, only whole gifts" (or "… only whole ones") as the text, and **:34** paraphrases it as "Heaven grants only complete gifts". "Only whole gifts" is the translator's gloss (plain type in the Davidson edition); the Aramaic is "a half, in Heaven they do not give". 327:154 gives the Aramaic correctly. [verified]
4. **327-symphyme.md:231** ("the organ can be narrowed, but only by maiming it, and the maiming is the price"): the text records no cost to the good from the blinding; Rashi calls it a small remedy that leaves other illicit arousal intact. [verified]
5. **327-symphyme.md:34** ("the prophet warns 'if you kill it the world will be destroyed'") holds for Yoma only; Sanhedrin 64a lacks the warning and the release. [verified]
6. **327-symphyme.md:42** presents 煩惱即菩提 among "Zhiyi's 即 propositions". Zhiyi uses it more than anyone in CBETA, but did not originate it: Jingying Huiyuan (523–592) quotes it as a sūtra line (T1851 juan 18, 828b09; T1776 juan 3, 484b16). (The earlier claim that it was "already in" the Chinese translations T1604 and T1596 was wrong: CBETA dates them 630–633 and 605–616, after Zhiyi.) [verified: CBETA]
7. **327-symphyme.md:50** ("Thamus rejects as a pharmakon that destroys memory"): a paraphrase, not Plato's wording. Thamus calls it "a pharmakon not of memory but of reminding" that "will produce forgetfulness … through neglect of memory" (*Phdr.* 275a). [verified: Perseus TEI]
8. **archaeology/navajo/hozho-cosmology-foundations.md:300–307**: "preserved by Changing Woman's intervention"; the list Old Age / Cold (*Dibáá'*) / Poverty / Lice; moral rationales. Not what Matthews 1897 ¶¶360–367 records (§1.8): there Changing Woman only withholds where three of them live, the beings are spared on their own pleas, Hunger stands where the repo has Lice, and the reasons are generative. The repo passage cites no source for this episode (the file names Zolbrod's 1984 *Diné bahane'* at :273 for the creation narrative in general, and Zolbrod was not opened here), so it may follow another telling; it should say which. [verified against Matthews; *dibáá'* = thirst is from memory]
9. **canon/core/emetme.md:60** cites "the Talmudic tradition asks 'what is God's seal?'" without a locus; Yoma 69b seg. 8 and Sanhedrin 64a seg. 5 supply one (not a contradiction; an omission). [verified]
