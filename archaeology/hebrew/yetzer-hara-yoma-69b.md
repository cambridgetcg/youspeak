# Yetzer ha-ra at Yoma 69b — the removal test that a tradition ran, and how it ended

_The forge at 327 cited Yoma 69b as its canonical witness and was refused partly because it stopped quoting one sentence before the end. This file opens the passage in full, from the outcry in Nehemiah 9:4 to the blinding and release, together with its parallel in Sanhedrin 64a, Rashi on both, and the midrash that calls the evil inclination "very good". The aim is narrow: to say exactly what the sources say, where the English translation adds to the Aramaic, and what the ending does to a reading of the passage as "you cannot remove the bad without losing the good". The yetzer belongs to a living tradition, so the first duty is in §0._

---

## 0. Protocol notice — read first

The yetzer ha-ra is not a museum term. It is a working category of Jewish ethics, prayer and law, taught today in yeshivot, in synagogue sermons and in Jewish-English everyday speech ("the yetzer hara got the better of me"). Its custodians are the rabbinic tradition and its living teachers, and the academic scholars of rabbinic literature.

1. **Custodians named as authorities.** The voices quoted here are the tradition's own: the Amoraim named in the Gemara (Rav, R. Yoḥanan, Rav Yehuda, Rav Yonatan, Rav/Rabbi Ḥanina), the Genesis Rabbah tradents (R. Naḥman bar Shmuel bar Naḥman in the name of R. Shmuel bar Naḥman), and Rashi. Modern scholarship is named in §3.8 and marked as from memory where it was not re-opened.
2. **Reception, not extraction.** The passage is a piece of aggadah, story-teaching that is part of Talmud study. This file reads its structure. It does not claim to state Jewish doctrine on evil, which is plural and argued across many texts.
3. **Consultation is desirable.** Before any coinage built on yetzer, or any liturgical or teaching use of this passage, review by a scholar of rabbinic literature is desirable. The forge at 327 already recorded the main hazard (327:99): a Jewishly literate reader will hear "yetzer" as "evil inclination" and will think they have understood.
4. **Retirement condition.** If custodians of the tradition ask, any coinage drawn from this file is withdrawn at once.

## 1. The concept in compact form

**Yetzer** (יֵצֶר, *yēṣer*) is a Hebrew noun from the root י־צ־ר *y-ṣ-r*, "to form, to fashion". In the Hebrew Bible it means what is formed or devised. Genesis 6:5 has *kol-yēṣer maḥšəḇōṯ libbō raq raʿ kol-hayyōm* (my literal rendering: "every devising of the thoughts of his heart was only evil all the time"; the JPS on Sefaria has "every plan devised by the human mind was nothing but evil all the time"). Genesis 8:21 has *yēṣer lēḇ hāʾāḏām raʿ minnəʿurāw* (literally "the devising of the human heart is evil from youth"; JPS "the devisings of the human mind are evil from youth"). Psalm 103:14 uses it without any moral colour: *kī-hūʾ yāḏaʿ yiṣrēnū*, rendered by the JPS translation on Sefaria "for [God] knows how we are formed" [verified: Sefaria, Psalms 103:14, Genesis 6:5, 8:21, Hebrew Masoretic text and JPS Gender-Sensitive Edition; the literal renderings are mine]. BDB (via the Sefaria lexicon API) gives יֵ֫צֶר as "form, framing, purpose", with Ps 103:14 under "form of man as made of the dust" and Gen 6:5, 8:21 under "imagination, device, purpose" [verified: https://www.sefaria.org/api/words/יצר]. The rabbinic tradition turns the biblical "devising of the heart" into a pair of drives, **yetzer ha-ra** (יֵצֶר הָרָע, the evil inclination) and **yetzer tov** (יֵצֶר טוֹב, the good inclination).

The rabbinic yetzer ha-ra is not simply bad. Genesis Rabbah 9:7 reads the "very good" of Genesis 1:31 as the evil inclination, because "were it not for the evil inclination, a man would never build a house, would never marry a wife, would never beget children, and would never engage in commerce" [verified: Sefaria, Bereshit Rabbah 9:7]. The Mishnah tells a person to love God "with both your inclinations, with the good inclination and with the evil inclination" (m. Berakhot 9:5) [verified: Sefaria]. The Talmud says that God created the evil inclination and created Torah as its *tavlin*, its spice or antidote (b. Kiddushin 30b) [verified: Sefaria]. So the tradition already holds a drive that is necessary, dangerous, to be enlisted rather than destroyed, and to be treated rather than removed.

Yoma 69b is where the tradition tells a story that tests this: the drive is captured, and the people must decide what to do with it.

**Structural properties (what any reader of this witness must preserve):**

1. **The test is run twice, and the results differ.** In the same story the inclination for idolatry (יִצְרָא דַעֲבוֹדָה זָרָה) is captured and sealed in a lead pot, and no loss to the world is recorded. The only price named is the petitioners' own: they renounce the reward that resisting it would have earned (seg. 6), and Rashi on Sanhedrin 64a reads Heaven's "truth" as assent "to this enactment, that it is good to remove it" (*מסכים אני עמהם בתקנה זו שטוב הדבר לסלקו*). The inclination for transgression (יִצְרָא דַעֲבֵירָה, understood as sexual from the context and from Rashi) is captured and imprisoned, and the world's fertility stops. The passage does not teach that every evil is bound to a good. It tells apart one drive that can be removed and one that cannot.
2. **Total removal costs the good, and the cost is observed.** During three days of imprisonment "they searched for a day-old egg in all the land of Israel and it was not found" (Yoma 69b).
3. **A clean split is refused.** "A half, in Heaven they do not give" (פַּלְגָא בִּרְקִיעָא לָא יָהֲבִי). Rashi explains what the "half" would have been: that the drive "should rule a person to cleave to his wife and not to another".
4. **A narrow human measure succeeds and is small.** They blinded its eyes and released it. It "was effective" in one thing: a person is not aroused toward a close kinswoman. Rashi on Sanhedrin 64a calls this "a small remedy" and lists what it leaves untouched (§3.4).

**Sibling distinctions.** Three Hebrew registers must be kept apart. *Yēṣer* in the Bible is a formed thing or a devising, morally neutral in Ps 103:14. *Yetzer ha-ra* in rabbinic Hebrew is a drive, named by its danger and praised for its necessity in the same breath (GenR 9:7). *Yitsra de-…* in the Babylonian Aramaic of Yoma names a drive by its object: idolatry, transgression. The objects matter, because the story treats them differently, and because the blinding later narrows one object while the drive stays.

## 3. Primary attestations and scholarly references

### 3.1 b. Yoma 69b, quoted to its end

Text: Sefaria, William Davidson Edition, vocalised Aramaic and English, fetched as JSON from `https://www.sefaria.org/api/texts/Yoma.69b`, segments 6–12, read through segment 21 [verified]. The Davidson English prints the literal translation in bold and adds explanation in plain type. I checked which words are bold: what is said below to be added is plain type there, and absent from the Aramaic.

**Outcry (seg. 6).** On Nehemiah 9:4, "and they cried with a loud voice to the LORD their God", Rav (some say R. Yoḥanan) says they said: *bīyā bīyā* ("woe, woe"). It is this that destroyed the Temple, burned its Sanctuary, killed the righteous and exiled Israel, *וַעֲדַיִין מְרַקֵּד בֵּינַן* "and it still dances among us". *כְּלוּם יְהַבְתֵּיהּ לַן אֶלָּא לְקַבּוֹלֵי בֵּיהּ אַגְרָא? לָא אִיהוּ בָּעֵינַן, וְלָא אַגְרֵיהּ בָּעֵינַן* "Did You give it to us only so that we receive reward through it? We want neither it nor its reward." The Davidson English adds "the evil inclination for idol worship"; the Aramaic says only "this". (Rashi supplies the same reading: they prayed for the idolatry yetzer to be handed over.) The petitioners already state a price they will pay: the reward that comes from resisting it.

**Answer and fast (seg. 7–9).** A note falls from heaven bearing *אֱמֶת*, "truth". Rav Ḥanina: "learn from this that the seal of the Holy One, blessed be He, is truth." They sit fasting three days and three nights and it is handed over. *נְפַק אֲתָא כִּי גוּרְיָא דְנוּרָא מִבֵּית קָדְשֵׁי הַקֳּדָשִׁים* "it came out like a cub of fire from the Holy of Holies." *אֲמַר לְהוּ נָבִיא לְיִשְׂרָאֵל: הַיְינוּ יִצְרָא דַעֲבוֹדָה זָרָה* "The prophet said to Israel: this is the inclination of idolatry", citing Zechariah 5:8, "and he said, this is wickedness". The Aramaic says only *nāḇī*, "the prophet"; the Davidson English adds "Zechariah", and Rashi identifies him as *זכריה בן עדו*, Zechariah son of Iddo.

**The idolatry inclination is sealed away (seg. 10).** When they seized it a hair fell from it and its shriek carried four hundred parasangs. They asked what to do in case Heaven should have mercy on it. The prophet: *שַׁדְיוּהוּ בְּדוּדָא דַאֲבָרָא … דַּאֲבָרָא מִשְׁאָב שָׁאֵיב קָלָא* "throw it into a pot of lead … for lead absorbs sound", with Zechariah 5:8, "and he cast a stone of lead upon its mouth". The Davidson English adds "They followed this advice and were freed of the evil inclination for idol worship"; the Aramaic implies the outcome through the verse but does not say it. No cost to the world is recorded.

**Second capture (seg. 11).** *אֲמַרוּ: הוֹאִיל וְעֵת רָצוֹן הוּא, נִבְעֵי רַחֲמֵי אַיִּצְרָא דַעֲבֵירָה* "They said: since it is a time of favour, let us pray for mercy concerning the inclination of transgression." They prayed and it was handed over. The Davidson English adds "the Sages" and "in the area of sexual relationships"; the Aramaic has "they" and *ʿaverah*, "transgression".

**The test, and the end (seg. 12), in full:**

> אֲמַר לְהוּ: חֲזוֹ, דְּאִי קָטְלִיתוּ לֵיהּ לְהָהוּא, כָּלֵי עָלְמָא. חַבְשׁוּהוּ תְּלָתָא יוֹמֵי, וּבָעוּ בֵּיעֲתָא בַּת יוֹמָא בְּכׇל אֶרֶץ יִשְׂרָאֵל וְלָא אִשְׁתְּכַח. אָמְרִי: הֵיכִי נַעֲבֵיד? נִקְטְלֵיהּ — כָּלֵי עָלְמָא, נִיבְעֵי רַחֲמֵי אַפַּלְגָא — פַּלְגָא בִּרְקִיעָא לָא יָהֲבִי. כַּחְלִינְהוּ לְעֵינֵיהּ וְשַׁבְקוּהוּ, וְאַהְנִי דְּלָא מִיגָּרֵי בֵּיהּ לְאִינִישׁ בְּקָרִיבְתֵּהּ.

My literal rendering, checked against the Davidson bold text, Rashi and Jastrow:

> He said to them: "See: if you kill that one, the world comes to an end." They imprisoned it three days, and they sought a day-old egg in all the land of Israel and it was not found. They said: "What shall we do? Shall we kill it? The world comes to an end. Shall we pray for mercy for a half? A half, in Heaven they do not give." They blinded its eyes and let it go. And it was effective in this: that a person is not aroused by it toward his kinswoman.

The Davidson English for the same lines reads: "Zechariah the prophet said to them: See and understand that if you kill this evil inclination the world will be destroyed because as a result there will also no longer be any desire to procreate. … They imprisoned it for three days. At that time, people searched for a fresh egg throughout all of Eretz Yisrael and could not find one. … If we pray for half … nothing will be achieved because Heaven does not grant half gifts, only whole gifts. What did they do? They gouged out its eyes, effectively limiting its power, and set it free. And this was effective to the extent that a person is no longer aroused to commit incest with his close relatives." In the plain (non-bold) type of that translation are: "Zechariah", "because … procreate", "gifts, only whole gifts", "effectively limiting its power" and the explanation of the eggs. They are the translator's, not the Gemara's. "Gouged out" renders *כַּחְלִינְהוּ*, which Jastrow gives as "to paint the eyes", used ironically for "to blind the eyes", and which Rashi explains as "they blinded its eyes by means of kohl" [verified: Jastrow and Rashi via Sefaria]. Jastrow's own English for the same verb form at Berakhot 58a is "his associates put his eyes out", so the Davidson "gouged" is not without lexical company. What it adds is a method: Rashi names kohl, and nothing in the Aramaic or the commentaries opened here mentions gouging. "Blinded" is the reading the sources support; "gouged" specifies more than they do.

The passage ends there. Segment 13 goes back to the Nehemiah exposition ("In the West they taught …"), and segment 14 says why the Men of the Great Assembly were given that name.

### 3.2 Rashi on Yoma 69b

Vilna text via the Sefaria v3 API, `Rashi_on_Yoma.69b` [verified]:

- *אמר להו נביא — זכריה בן עדו* — the prophet is Zechariah son of Iddo.
- *כליא עלמא — יכלה העולם שלא תהא פריה ורביה* — "the world would end, for there would be no procreation". So the Davidson gloss "because … procreate" (plain type, §3.1) is not in the Gemara, but it is Rashi's reading.
- *ביעתא בת יומא — ביצה הנולדה בו ביום לפי שפסק החימום מן המעים ואפילו אותן שעיברה התרנגולת כבר אינן מתבשלין במעיה* — "an egg laid that same day; for the heat had ceased from the innards, and even those the hen had already conceived did not ripen inside her." What stops is generative heat itself, in animals as well as people.
- *לפלגא — שיהא שולט באדם ליזקק לאשתו ולא לאחרת* — the "half" they would have asked for: "that it should rule a person to cleave to his wife and not to another". This is exactly the clean split between the licit and illicit uses of one drive, and Heaven refuses it.
- *כחלינהו — סימו את עיניו ע"י כחול* — "they blinded its eyes by means of kohl".

### 3.3 The parallel at b. Sanhedrin 64a

Sefaria, William Davidson Edition, `Sanhedrin.64a`, segments 3–9 [verified]. The setting is different: the Gemara is asking whether Israel worshipped idols for their own sake or only to permit forbidden unions (seg. 3, "after they had become attached to it"). The two tellings differ in ways that matter:

| Feature | Yoma 69b | Sanhedrin 64a |
|---|---|---|
| Tradent of the outcry | Rav, some say R. Yoḥanan | Rav Yehuda, some say Rav Yonatan |
| "Seal is truth" | Rav Ḥanina | Rabbi Ḥanina |
| Prophet's warning "if you kill it, the world ends" | present | **absent** |
| Egg | *בְּכׇל אֶרֶץ יִשְׂרָאֵל* "in all the land of Israel" | *לְחוֹלֶה* "for a sick person" |
| Deliberation | kill it? (world ends) / pray for half? (not given) | pray for half? (not given) only |
| Half | *פַּלְגָא בִּרְקִיעָא לָא יָהֲבִי* "in Heaven" | *פַּלְגָא מֵרְקִיעָא לָא יָהֲבִי* "from Heaven" |
| Blinding | *כַּחְלִינְהוּ לְעֵינֵיהּ וְשַׁבְקוּהוּ* "blinded … and let it go" | *כַּחְלִינְהוּ לְעֵינֵיהּ* "blinded" (**no release stated**) |
| Effect | *וְאַהְנִי דְּלָא מִיגָּרֵי בֵּיהּ לְאִינִישׁ בְּקָרִיבְתֵּהּ* | *אַהֲנִי בֵּיהּ דְּלָא מִיגָּרֵי אִינִישׁ בְּקָרִיבְתֵּיהּ* |

So the "if you kill it the world ends" warning and the explicit release belong to Yoma's telling, not to both. A reader who quotes "the Talmud" for these should cite Yoma 69b.

### 3.4 Rashi on Sanhedrin 64a — "a small remedy"

In Yoma the identification is also implied by the sugya's own context (see §3.7); Rashi states it.
- *לבעו פלגא — שלא יהא שולט במקום עבירה כדי שלא יהא תובע עריות* — the half: "that it should not rule where there is transgression, so that it would not seek forbidden unions."
- *כחלינהו לעיניה — סמו עיניו בכחול* — "they blinded its eyes with kohl."

### 3.5 Genesis Rabbah 9:7 — "very good: this is the evil inclination"

Sefaria, *Bereshit Rabbah* 9:7, Hebrew (Midrash Rabbah, TE) and English (The Sefaria Midrash Rabbah, 2022) [verified]:

> רַבִּי נַחְמָן בַּר שְׁמוּאֵל בַּר נַחְמָן בְּשֵׁם רַב שְׁמוּאֵל בַּר נַחְמָן אָמַר, הִנֵּה טוֹב מְאֹד, זֶה יֵצֶר טוֹב. וְהִנֵּה טוֹב מְאֹד, זֶה יֵצֶר רָע. וְכִי יֵצֶר הָרָע טוֹב מְאֹד, אֶתְמְהָא. אֶלָּא שֶׁאִלּוּלֵי יֵצֶר הָרָע לֹא בָּנָה אָדָם בַּיִת, וְלֹא נָשָׂא אִשָּׁה, וְלֹא הוֹלִיד, וְלֹא נָשָׂא וְנָתַן. וְכֵן שְׁלֹמֹה אוֹמֵר (קהלת ד, ד): כִּי הִיא קִנְאַת אִישׁ מֵרֵעֵהוּ.

> "'Behold it was very good' — this is the good inclination; 'and behold it was very good' — this is the evil inclination. Is the evil inclination, then, very good? Rather, were it not for the evil inclination, a man would never build a house, would never marry a wife, would never beget children, and would never engage in commerce. Likewise, Solomon says: '… that it is each man's envy of his counterpart' (Ecclesiastes 4:4)."

The exegesis reads the extra *vav* of *וְהִנֵּה* in Gen 1:31 as adding a second "very good". The list has four goods, not three: building, marrying, begetting, and trade (*נָשָׂא וְנָתַן*). The proof text names the engine as *קִנְאָה*, envy or rivalry, not only sexual desire. The midrash first states an identity ("very good" *is* the evil inclination) and then explains it by causes (without it, no house).

### 3.6 Further rabbinic and biblical context

- **m. Berakhot 9:5** [verified: Sefaria, Torat Emet text; Davidson English]: *בְּכָל לְבָבְךָ, בִּשְׁנֵי יְצָרֶיךָ, בְּיֵצֶר טוֹב וּבְיֵצֶר רָע* "'with all your heart' — with your two inclinations, with the good inclination and with the evil inclination." ### 3.7 What the passage concludes, and what that does to a "without cost" reading

**What the passage concludes.** The yetzer of transgression was neither killed nor halved. It was blinded and released (Yoma; Sanhedrin states the blinding but not the release). The text names one effect: a person is no longer aroused toward a close kinswoman. Nothing more is stated. The text does not say that the eggs came back, that procreation went on, or that the drive was weakened in general. Those follow from the frame (the reason not to kill it was to keep the world) and from the release, but they are inferences. Rashi on Sanhedrin is the explicit witness that the drive kept working: the remedy was "small", and arousal toward a married woman and toward a menstruant remained.

**A common recollection of the passage, checked.** "They do not simply release it but blind it, which leaves a diminished but working drive": **confirmed in substance, with two refinements.** (a) "Diminished" is broader than the sources. Nothing says the drive was weakened. What is described is one vector closed (close kin), with the drive's force and its other illicit vectors untouched (Rashi, Sanhedrin 64a). "Retargeted at one point" fits the text better than "diminished". (b) "Working" is Rashi's explicit statement and the Yoma frame's implication. It is not in the Gemara's own words.

**Other details of the usual summary, checked against the text and against 327:**

- **"The Men of the Great Assembly capture the inclination":** not stated in the capture narrative, but supported by its context. The narrative's actors are "they", the people of the outcry in Nehemiah 9:4 (in the verse, the Levites on the platform). Yoma goes on (seg. 13–14) expounding the same Nehemiah 9 prayer, at 9:32 ("the great, the mighty and the awesome God"), and there R. Yehoshua ben Levi explains why "they" are called the Men of the Great Assembly. The sugya therefore identifies the people of Nehemiah 9 as the Men of the Great Assembly, and Rashi on Sanhedrin 64a makes it explicit for 9:4 [verified: Sefaria, Yoma 69b seg. 13–14; Nehemiah 9:4, 9:32; Rashi on Sanhedrin 64a]. The brief's wording is a fair reading, though the Gemara never says "the Men of the Great Assembly captured it" in so many words.
- **"Three days in which no fresh egg could be found in the land":** confirmed for Yoma ("in all the land of Israel"). Sanhedrin has "for a sick person" instead. There are two separate three-day periods: the fast (seg. 9) and the imprisonment (seg. 12).
- **"Heaven grants only complete gifts":** the words "only whole gifts" are the translator's. The Aramaic says "a half, in Heaven they do not give". 327 quotes the gloss as text at :178, :181, :193, :202, :212, :218 and :227, and paraphrases it at :34 (its Aramaic at :154 is correct).
- **"Gouged out its eyes":** the translator's word (quoted at 327:212, :214, :218, :220, :227, :235). The Aramaic verb means "blinded" (Jastrow, an ironic use of "paint the eyes"; at Ber 58a he renders it "put his eyes out"), and Rashi says the blinding was done with kohl. Nothing opened here mentions gouging.
- **"The eggs return" / "The eggs come back" / "with procreation preserved"** (327:212, :229, :178): inferences, not text.

**What this does to a "without cost" reading.** "Non-separability without cost" means: the bad cannot be removed without the good paying for it. The passage gives five results, not one:

| Operation | Agent | Result in the text | Bearing on "no separation without cost" |
|---|---|---|---|
| Seal away the idolatry inclination | human, on the prophet's advice | done; no cost to the world recorded; the petitioners had already renounced the reward for resisting it (seg. 6) | **Against a general claim.** The same story removes one evil inclination with no loss to any good except that renounced reward. The region is not "every evil is bound to a good". It must name the drive that fails this test. |
| Kill the transgression inclination | (not done) | "the world comes to an end" (warning, Yoma only) | For, as a stated consequence, not an observed one. |
| Imprison it (total suspension) | human | no day-old egg in the land; Rashi: generative heat stopped | **For, and observed.** This is the only time the experiment is actually run, and the good goes dark with the bad. |
| Pray for a half (clean split, licit only) | divine | refused: "a half, in Heaven they do not give" | **For.** There is no clean partition from above. |
| Blind and release | human | effective against one vector (close kin); a "small remedy"; other illicit vectors remain (Rashi) | **Against strict co-variance; neutral on cost.** A narrow reduction of the shadow is achieved and no cost to the good is recorded. The price falls on the drive's sight, not on the world's fertility. What the text does show is that the gain is small. |

So the passage supports a three-part claim, not a slogan: **wholesale removal costs the good; a clean split is unavailable; narrow pruning at the edge is available, cheap, and small.** It does not support "any reduction of the exposure is paid for out of the capacity". The forge's own refined verdict, "the organ can be narrowed, but only by maiming it, and the maiming is the price" (327:231), reads a cost into the blinding that the text does not record. If the blinding counts as a cost, the cost is borne by the drive, and the effect on the good is silence, not evidence. Whatever the text costs, it costs in **scope**: you can close one door, not the room.

**Shape.** Of the three shapes separated in the companion convergence notes (mixed now and separable later; one root bearing two fruits; one thing under two descriptions), Yoma 69b is **one root bearing two fruits**, with the fruits distinguished by object. The root (the drive, which in Rashi's reading is the generative heat of living things) cannot be cut without losing the good fruit. The fruits are not one thing under two names, because the blinding pruned one object (close kin) while others remained, and an identity reading would forbid that. They are not two plants mixed and sorted later either, since nothing in the story ever separates them. Genesis Rabbah 9:7 states an identity ("very good" is the evil inclination) and defends it with the root-and-fruits argument (without it, no house).

## 4. Etymology and family

- **Root.** י־צ־ר *y-ṣ-r*, "to form, fashion" (the verb *yāṣar*; the participle *yōṣēr*, "potter"). BDB s.v. יָצַר: "form, fashion (NH in pt. יוֹצֵר potter, creator …; Ph. יצר potter; … Syriac ܝܰܨܪܳܐ = יֵצֶר …; Assyrian êṣêru)", with the potter forming a vessel out of clay among the senses [verified: Sefaria lexicon API, BDB]. The cognates are BDB's listing; they were not checked in a Semitic etymological dictionary.
- **Morphology.** *yēṣer* is a segolate noun with penultimate stress (*YE-tser*; BDB prints יֵ֫צֶר with the accent on the first syllable) [verified: BDB via Sefaria]. The Aramaic *yiṣrā* is the emphatic state, stressed on the final syllable in the conventional Israeli reading [from memory: not re-checked]. *Raʿ* (רָע) "bad, evil" and *ṭôḇ* (טוֹב) "good" are adjectives. *Ha-ra* takes the article in *yetzer ha-ra*. The pairing in the Mishnah and in Genesis Rabbah is the articleless *yetzer tov / yetzer raʿ*, but Genesis Rabbah 9:7 then twice says *yetzer ha-ra* with the article in the same passage (Sefaria's printed text; manuscripts not checked).
- **Doctrinal family inside the tradition.** *Yetzer* presupposes *lev* (the heart, the seat of devising, Gen 6:5; 8:21). It is set against Torah as *tavlin* (Kid 30b) and is enlisted in love (m. Ber 9:5). It is personified in the Bavli as a creature that can be captured, a "cub of fire", that shrieks, can be imprisoned and can be blinded. The personification is what lets the story run a removal test at all.

## 5. The unnamed region — the gap-shadows

The forge at 327 gathered the circumlocution evidence and the near-neighbours (327:81–133). This file adds what the full passage shows about the region's edges.

- English speakers can say "you can't have one without the other", "necessary evil" and "the defects of one's qualities", but none of these separates the three outcomes the passage separates: total removal (costly), clean split (unavailable), narrow pruning (possible and small).
- Ordinary practice already runs Yoma's discrimination without naming it: some vices go into the lead pot at no recorded cost to the world (idolatry here), and some cannot be sealed away without the world going dark (desire here).

Near-misses, each failing:

| Candidate | Why it fails |
|---|---|
| *necessary evil* (English) | It treats the evil as a separate means to a good end. Yoma's drive is one root of both, and the passage shows that "the evil" can be pruned at an edge, which a "necessary means" reading cannot express. |
| *felix culpa* (Latin liturgical) | A past fault redeemed by what followed: sequential, about history, not about a standing constitution. |
| *trade-off* (English) | A dial between two separately specified goods. Yoma refuses the dial ("no half") and still allows edge-pruning, which is neither a dial setting nor a refusal. |
| *yetzer ha-ra* itself | Names the drive by its bad pole and is a live term in Jewish English (327:99). It is the witness, not a substitute. |
| Tiantai 性惡 *xìng'è* (Chinese Buddhist) | Says the evil in the nature cannot be cut off at all, while cultivated evil (修惡) is cut off entirely (觀音玄義 T1726). Read on, the passage adds that the Buddha, unstained, "broadly uses the dharma-gates of evil to transform beings". That is stronger than Yoma on non-excisability, and it has no cost structure: what can be removed is removed whole, and what remains cannot be removed. Witness, not substitute. |
