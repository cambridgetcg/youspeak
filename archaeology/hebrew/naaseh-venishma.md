# Naʿaśeh ve-nishmaʿ — "we will do and we will hear"

_The forge target wire-task-accept names its register as "na'aseh v'nishma", and its sketched donor pair begins `hebrew/shema-uptake`. No archaeology file of that name exists, and the canon's only š-m-ʿ word, shemme, names a state that arrives (canon/core/shemme.md), not an act someone performs. This dig opens the verse the target points to and reads its reception to the end. The finding that matters most for a later forge is that the tradition's own word for the act is not š-m-ʿ at all. It is q-b-l, "to receive, accept, take upon oneself". The Aramaic Targum translates nishmaʿ with it, the Mishnah uses it to explain why the Shema's paragraphs come in their order, and the Talmud's sharpest critic of naʿaśeh ve-nishmaʿ uses it to say what Israel should have done instead. The dig also reports what complicates the praise. On the same Talmudic page the acceptance is coerced, renewed only centuries later, broken within weeks, and mocked by an outsider as rash._

## 1. The concept in compact form

At Sinai the people answer Moses three times. At Exodus 19:8, before the Decalogue, they say *kol ʾăšer dibber YHWH naʿăśeh*, "all that YHWH has spoken we will do" [verified: sefaria.org/api/texts/Exodus.19.8, Miqra according to the Masorah]. At 24:3, after Moses tells them the words and the judgments, they again say only *naʿăśeh* [verified: sefaria.org/api/texts/Exodus.24.3-8]. At 24:7, after Moses "took the book of the covenant and read it in the ears of the people" (*wayyiqraʾ bəʾozne hāʿām*), they say:

> וַיֹּאמְרוּ כֹּל אֲשֶׁר־דִּבֶּר יְהֹוָה נַעֲשֶׂה וְנִשְׁמָע
> *wayyōʾmərū kōl ʾăšer-dibber YHWH naʿăśeh wə-nišmāʿ*
> "and they said: all that YHWH has spoken we will do and we will hear"
> [verified: sefaria.org/api/texts/Exodus.24.3-8, Hebrew per Miqra according to the Masorah; the translation is mine and literal]

The covenant is then sealed with blood dashed on the people (24:8). The phrase is two first-person-plural imperfects joined by *wə-*: *naʿăśeh* from ʿ-ś-h "to do, make" and *nišmāʿ* from š-m-ʿ "to hear". The second word is added only at the third answer, after the book has been read aloud. So the people say "we will hear" just after they have heard. That suggests (my inference from the verified text, not a source's claim) that *nišmāʿ* here means more than perceiving sound: heed, obey, understand, or accept.

The phrase entered Jewish thought as the paradigm of commitment given before understanding is complete. b. Shabbat 88a calls it a "secret the ministering angels use". The same page also holds the strongest counter-tradition in rabbinic literature: the mountain held over the people like a cask.

**Structural properties the forge must preserve, or consciously decline:**

1. **Acceptance is spoken as an answer.** It is not a feeling or a state. It is a sentence said back to a speaker ("and they said …"), in the first person plural, about the future. It is performative: the covenant ritual (24:8) follows it.
2. **The second verb carries the uptake.** In the Targum's rendering (§3) *nišmāʿ* becomes *nəqabbel*, "we will accept". The hearing is the accepting.
3. **The order is contested, even inside the Bible.** Deuteronomy 5:24 reverses it: *wə-šāmaʿnū wə-ʿāśīnū*, "we will hear and we will do" [verified: sefaria.org/api/texts/Deuteronomy.5.24]. The rabbinic praise rests on Exodus's order alone.
4. **The acceptance is not plainly free.** b. Shabbat 88a sets beside it the mountain held overhead ("if you accept the Torah, good; if not, there will be your burial") and records it as a legal "caveat" to the Torah's binding force (§3).

**Sibling distinctions.** Three Hebrew words share this ground and must not be blurred:

- **šāmaʿ** is reception through the ear that can extend to heeding and obeying. Lexically it is one verb; the senses run from "perceive by ear" to "consent, agree" and "obey" (BDB). It names hearing that may become yes.
- **qibbēl** is the act of taking something that faces you and making it yours. Its root sense is "to be opposite" (Klein: "from this orig. meaning developed the meanings 'to go, to meet, encounter; to take that which is opposite; to take over; to receive'") [verified: Klein s.v. קבל I]. It names the yes as an act, often with *ʿal*, "upon oneself" (Est 9:27; m. Ber 2:2).
- **ʿāśāh** is the doing that fulfils. In this verse it comes first.

The canon word shemme (canon/core/shemme.md) already holds the first register, as "receptive hearing as a received gift … pre-volitional". The forge target asks for the second.

Two readings stand out for a forge. **The Targum tradition hears š-m-ʿ as q-b-l.** It does so in Exodus's order and in Deuteronomy's alike, so for the Aramaic translators the second verb was "accept" whichever way round it stood. Jastrow records the Targumic idiom *qabbēl lə-mēmrā*, "to accept [the] word" = "to consent, listen to, obey" (Targ. Gen 3:17, 16:2; Targ. Deut 26:17) [verified: Jastrow s.v. קְבַל, via sefaria.org/api/words/קבל]. **Modern Jewish translation (NJPS) dissolves the order problem altogether** by reading one act, "faithfully do".

### 3.2 The medieval readers

- **Rashbam** dissolves the paradox by tense: "*naʿăśeh*: we will do what He has spoken; *wə-gam nišmaʿ*: and also we will hear what He commands us from now on, and we will fulfil it" (נעשה מה שדיבר וגם נשמע מה שיצונו עוד מכאן ולהבא ונקיים) [verified: sefaria.org/api/texts/Rashbam_on_Exodus.24.7, Hebrew; the translation is mine]. "Doing" covers the words already read; "hearing" is the open-ended acceptance of words not yet spoken. On this reading the binding part is the second verb.
- **Sforno** insists on informed consent. Moses read it in their ears "so that they would know what they were accepting upon themselves, so that they would not be as ones mistaken" (שֶׁיֵּדְעוּ מָה הֵם מְקַבְּלִים עֲלֵיהֶם, שֶׁלֹּא יִהְיוּ כְּמוּטְעִים). He reads *naʿăśeh wə-nišmāʿ* as "we will act so as to obey His voice, like servants serving the master not for the sake of receiving reward", citing Ps 103:20 [verified: sefaria.org/api/texts/Sforno_on_Exodus.24.7, Hebrew; the translation is mine]. Sforno's gloss on the reading uses *məqabbəlīm*: hearing is the condition of a valid *qabbālāh*.
- **Ibn Ezra**: "when he read [it] in the ears of the elders and they accepted it upon themselves (וקבלו על נפשם)". The blood was sprinkled on the elders, who stood for all Israel [verified: sefaria.org/api/texts/Ibn_Ezra_on_Exodus.24.7]. (Sefaria files this comment at 24:7, but its lemma ויקח and its content, the sprinkling of the blood, belong to 24:8.)

### 3.3 b. Shabbat 88a–b, read to its end

Text: the William Davidson edition (vocalized Aramaic and Steinsaltz-based English) [verified: sefaria.org/api/texts/Shabbat.88a and Shabbat.88b, segments 88a.5–88b.8]. The translations below are mine and literal. The Davidson English adds explanatory words throughout; I flag the additions that bear on this reading, not every one.

**(a) The mountain like a cask (88a.5).** On Ex 19:17 *wayyityaṣṣəbū bə-taḥtīt hā-hār* ("they stood at the foot of the mountain", [verified: sefaria.org/api/texts/Exodus.19.17]):

> אָמַר רַב אַבְדִּימִי בַּר חָמָא בַּר חַסָּא: מְלַמֵּד שֶׁכָּפָה הַקָּדוֹשׁ בָּרוּךְ הוּא עֲלֵיהֶם אֶת הָהָר כְּגִיגִית, וְאָמַר לָהֶם: אִם אַתֶּם מְקַבְּלִים הַתּוֹרָה מוּטָב, וְאִם לָאו — שָׁם תְּהֵא קְבוּרַתְכֶם.
> "Rav Avdimi bar Ḥama bar Ḥasa said: [the verse] teaches that the Holy One, blessed be He, overturned the mountain over them like a *gīgīt* [Mod. reading [giˈgit]], and said to them: if you accept (*məqabbəlīm*) the Torah, good; and if not, there will be your burial."

Rashi glosses *gīgīt* as "a vat in which they put beer" (קובא שמטילין בה שכר) and *taḥtīt* as "literally under the mountain" [verified: sefaria.org/api/texts/Rashi_on_Shabbat.88a.5]. The coercion is phrased with **q-b-l**.

> אָמַר רַב אַחָא בַּר יַעֲקֹב: מִכָּאן מוֹדָעָא רַבָּה לְאוֹרָיְיתָא.
> "Rav Aḥa bar Yaʿakov said: from here [comes] a great *mōdāʿā* [a legal protest-notice; Mod. reading [modaˈʔa ˈraba]] against the Torah."

Rashi: "if He summons them to judgment, 'why did you not fulfil what you accepted upon yourselves (*qibbaltem ʿălēkhem*)?', they have an answer: they accepted it under duress (*bə-ʾōnes*)" [verified: Rashi on Shabbat 88a:5].

> אָמַר רָבָא: אַף עַל פִּי כֵן הֲדוּר קַבְּלוּהָ בִּימֵי אֲחַשְׁוֵרוֹשׁ, דִּכְתִיב: ״קִיְּמוּ וְקִבְּלוּ הַיְּהוּדִים״ — קִיְּימוּ מַה שֶּׁקִּיבְּלוּ כְּבָר.
> "Rava said: even so, they accepted it again (*hădūr qabbəlūhā*) in the days of Ahasuerus, as it is written: 'the Jews upheld and accepted' (Est 9:27) — they upheld what they had already accepted."

The Davidson English renders the last clause "ordained what they had already taken upon themselves *through coercion at Sinai*". The words "through coercion at Sinai" are an explanatory addition; they are not in the Aramaic. Rashi says the renewal came "from love of the miracle that was done for them" (מאהבת הנס) [verified].

**(b) Tosafot on the same line** [verified: sefaria.org/api/texts/Tosafot_on_Shabbat.88a.5]:
- Why hold up a mountain if they had already said *naʿăśeh* before *nišmāʿ*? "Perhaps they would retract when they saw the great fire, at which their souls departed."
- How does this square with b. Avodah Zarah 2b? There the nations plead "did You overturn a mountain over *us* like a cask?". Tosafot take this to imply that had He coerced them, they would have had no answer: a coerced acceptance would still have bound them. Yet here coercion yields a *mōdāʿā*. Answer: the *mōdāʿā* concerns not having *kept* the Torah ("for not having kept it there is an answer"), not having *accepted* it. Coercion excuses failure to keep, not failure to accept.
- Rabbenu Tam: the covenant Moses made with Israel over the Torah and its commandments, with an oath (b. Nedarim 25a), and the acceptance of the whole Torah at Mount Gerizim and Mount Ebal (b. Sotah 37b), were "by way of the divine utterance, so it is as though against their will". Only in Ahasuerus's day "did they accept it from their own knowing (*mi-daʿtām*), out of love of the miracle". Tosafot add that Joshua's covenant (Josh 24) bound them only not to serve idols. [Nedarim, Sotah and Avodah Zarah are cited here as Tosafot gives them; I did not open those folios. Tosafot do not name Moab; placing the Nedarim covenant there is from memory: not re-checked.]

So by the reckoning of the Talmud's own glossators, the fully free acceptance came centuries after Sinai, at Purim. The Sinai acceptance was praised and also, in law, defective.

**(c) The whole world conditional on the acceptance (88a.6).** Ḥizkiya reads Ps 76:9, "the earth feared and was still". Reish Lakish explains: the extra definite article in *yōm ha-šiššī* (Gen 1:31) teaches that God made a condition with the works of creation: "if Israel accept (*məqabbəlīm*) the Torah, you endure; if not, I return you to chaos (*tōhū wā-bōhū*)" [verified: 88a.6]. (The Davidson English adds "on the sixth day of Sivan", which is Rashi's gloss, not the Aramaic.)

**(d) The praise of the order (88a.7–8).**
- R. Simai: "when Israel put *naʿăśeh* before *nišmāʿ*, 600,000 ministering angels came and tied two crowns on each one, one for *naʿăśeh* and one for *nišmāʿ*." When they sinned [the Golden Calf], 1,200,000 angels of destruction came down and removed them (Ex 33:6). Reish Lakish: God will return them (Isa 35:10) [verified: 88a.7].
- R. Elazar: "when Israel put *naʿăśeh* before *nišmāʿ*, a heavenly voice went out and said to them: who revealed to My children this secret (*rāz*) that the ministering angels use?" The proof is Ps 103:20: *ʿōśē dəbārō lišmōaʿ bə-qōl dəbārō*, "doers of His word, to hear the voice of His word": first doing, then hearing [verified: 88a.8; Ps 103:20 verified separately]. Rashi on the verse: "ready to do before they hear, and not in the way of other servants, who first hear the matter to know whether they can accept it upon themselves (*lǝqabbǝlān ʿălēhem*) or not" [verified: sefaria.org/api/texts/Rashi_on_Shabbat.88a, gloss at 88a.8]. Rashi's "other servants" state the min's position before the min speaks.
- R. Ḥama b. R. Ḥanina on Song 2:3, "like an apple tree": its fruit comes before its leaves [verified: 88a.8]. The botany belongs to the homily; I have not checked it.

**(e) The outsider's critique (88a.9–88b.1).** This is the passage that matters most for a forge:

> הָהוּא מִינָא … אֲמַר לֵיהּ: עַמָּא פְּזִיזָא, דְּקַדְּמִיתוּ פּוּמַּיְיכוּ לְאוּדְנַיְיכוּ, אַכַּתִּי בְּפַחְזוּתַיְיכוּ קָיְימִיתוּ. בְּרֵישָׁא אִיבַּעְיָא לְכוּ לְמִשְׁמַע, אִי מָצִיתוּ — קַבְּלִיתוּ, וְאִי לָא — לָא קַבְּלִיתוּ.
> "A certain *mīnā* [sectarian/heretic] [who saw Rava so absorbed in study that he sat on his own fingers and pressed them until they bled] said to him: hasty people (*ʿammā pəzīzā* [Mod. reading ʔaˈma peziˈza]), who put your mouths before your ears! You still persist in your haste. First you ought to have listened (*lə-mišmaʿ*); if you were able (*māṣītū*), you would have accepted (*qabbəlītū*); and if not, you would not have accepted (*lā qabbəlītū*)."

*māṣītū* and *qabbəlītū* are perfects, 2nd person plural: the min describes what a prudent people would have done, not an imperative. (The Davidson English turns it into advice: "accept them … do not accept them".) Rashi glosses the min's charge: "before you heard how hard it is, and whether you could withstand it, you accepted it upon yourselves to fulfil it" [verified: Rashi on Shabbat 88a, gloss at 88a.9].

Rava answers with Prov 11:3: "we who walk in wholeness (*bi-šlīmūtā*), of us it is written 'the integrity of the upright guides them'; of those who walk in crookedness, 'the perverseness of the treacherous destroys them'" [verified: 88a.9–88b.1].

The critic's alternative is plain informed consent, stated with the same two roots: hear (š-m-ʿ), then accept (q-b-l) or decline. His sentence even includes the decline: *lā qabbəlītū*, "you would not have accepted". Rava does not deny that the order is unusual. He answers with a claim about the integrity of the relationship, not about what was known beforehand. *Šəlēmūt*, wholeness, is what makes acceptance before knowledge safe.

**(f) After the praise (88b.2–8).**
- R. Shmuel bar Naḥmani in the name of R. Yonatan, on Song 4:9 ("you have captured my heart with one of your eyes"): "at first, with one of your eyes; when you do (*li-kšeʾtaʿăśī*), with both of your eyes." The Davidson English adds "when you … merely accepted the Torah". The Aramaic says only "at first" [verified: 88b.2]. Acceptance is the half; the doing completes it.
- Ulla: "shameless is the bride who plays the harlot within her own wedding canopy." Rav Mari cites Song 1:12. The Golden Calf follows within weeks of the acceptance. Rav: "still, His affection is with us", because the verse says "gave [its fragrance]" and not "stank" [verified: 88b.2].
- A baraita: "those who are insulted and do not insult, who hear their disgrace and do not reply (*šōməʿīn ḥerpātān wə-ʾēnān məšībīn*), who act out of love and rejoice in suffering", of them Judges 5:31 speaks [verified: 88b.2].
- Each divine utterance split into seventy languages (Ps 68:12; Jer 23:29). At each utterance Israel's souls departed and were revived with the dew of resurrection; at each one they recoiled twelve mil and the angels walked them back [verified: 88b.3–6].
- The angels object to Moses ascending: "what is one born of woman doing among us?" God answers: *lə-qabbēl tōrāh bāʾ*, "he has come to receive the Torah" [verified: 88b.7]. Moses wins the argument by showing that the Torah's commands fit only creatures who were enslaved in Egypt, live among idolaters, and so on [verified: 88b.8, read as far as the segment's end; the Moses–angels dialogue continues on 89a, which I did not open].

**What complicates "free acceptance".** Read to its end, the sugya does not praise free acceptance before knowledge without reservation. It holds five things at once:
1. the order of the verbs as an angelic secret;
2. a mountain held overhead, with a legal caveat that follows from it;
3. a renewal of the acceptance centuries later, the first fully free one by Tosafot's reckoning;
4. a breach of the acceptance within weeks, in the bridal image;
5. an outsider's case for hear-first-then-accept, answered by an appeal to the integrity of the relationship rather than by argument.

A forge that takes "na'aseh v'nishma register" as its realm feature inherits all five, or must say which it drops.

### 3.4 Shema as the act of acceptance (m. Berakhot 2:2)

> אָמַר רַבִּי יְהוֹשֻׁעַ בֶּן קָרְחָה, לָמָּה קָדְמָה שְׁמַע לִוְהָיָה אִם שָׁמֹעַ, אֶלָּא כְדֵי שֶׁיְּקַבֵּל עָלָיו עֹל מַלְכוּת שָׁמַיִם תְּחִלָּה, וְאַחַר כָּךְ יְקַבֵּל עָלָיו עֹל מִצְוֹת.
> "Rabbi Yehoshua ben Korḥa said: why does [the section] *Šəmaʿ* precede *wə-hāyāh ʾim šāmōaʿ*? So that one should accept upon himself (*yəqabbēl ʿālāw*) the yoke of the kingdom of heaven first, and afterwards accept upon himself the yoke of the commandments."
> [verified: sefaria.org/api/texts/Mishnah_Berakhot.2.2, Hebrew per Torat Emet; the translation is mine]

The Mishnah itself joins š-m-ʿ and q-b-l: reciting "Hear, O Israel" is itself an act of acceptance (*qabbālāh*). The order matches the logic of *naʿăśeh wə-nišmāʿ*: one accepts the sovereign first, then the specific commands. Jastrow also cites b. Berakhot 13a for reciting the Shema "in any language that you understand (*šōmēaʿ*)". There "hear" means understand [verified: Jastrow s.v. שָׁמַע, as quoted in the Sefaria lexicon; Berakhot 13a itself not opened].

### 3.5 Esther 9:23, 27 — the biblical home of *qibbēl* as "undertake"

- 9:23 *wə-qibbēl ha-yəhūdīm ʾēt ʾăšer-hēḥēllū laʿăśōt*, "the Jews took on as an obligation what they had begun to do" (JPS 2023: "assumed as an obligation") [verified: sefaria.org/api/texts/Esther.9.23].
- 9:27 *qiyyəmū wə-qibbəlū ha-yəhūdīm ʿălēhem wə-ʿal zarʿām*, "the Jews upheld and took upon themselves and upon their descendants". The ketiv is singular וקבל, the qere plural וְקִבְּלוּ [verified: sefaria.org/api/texts/Esther.9.27]. *qiyyəmū* Tib. [qijjaˈmuː], Mod. [kijˈmu].

Esther is the verse Rava uses to say that the Torah was accepted again (§3.3a). In it, q-b-l joined with *ʿal*, "upon", is the Hebrew Bible's clearest way of saying "to bind oneself by accepting".

## 4. Etymology and family

- **š-m-ʿ** is pan-Semitic. BDB s.v. שָׁמַע lists Phoenician שמע, "Assyrian šemǔ", Arabic سَمِعَ, Sabaean סמע, Ethiopic ሰምዐ and Aramaic שְׁמַע. It also cites the Mesha Inscription line 28 משמעת, "obedience" [verified: BDB s.v. שָׁמַע, sefaria.org/api/words/שמע]. Klein lists for the Qal "he hearkened, listened to" and "he fulfilled one's advice", and for the Niphal "was understood" and "was obeyed" [verified: Klein]. So the step from hearing to obeying is already inside the lexeme. (Akkadian šemû: Wiktionary gives the spelling še-mu-u₂, the logograms GIŠ.TUK and ŠE.GA, and the senses "hear; listen, pay attention; obey", citing CAD s.v. šemû [verified: en.wiktionary.org/wiki/šemûm, the lemma page, which lists šemû as the non-mimated form; the URL wiki/šemû itself returns "not found"; CAD itself not opened]. IPA [ʃemuː] in conventional Assyriological reading.)
- **ʿ-ś-h**, by contrast, is narrowly Canaanite. BDB: "not Aramaic, and not certainly found in S[outh] Sem[itic] lang[uages]", attested in Moabite (Mesha Inscription) [verified: BDB s.v. עָשָׂה I]. That is why the Targum must render it with ʿ-b-d (*naʿbēd*).
- **q-b-l** is, by BDB's analysis, **late in Hebrew and an Aramaic loan-word**. It appears in Esther, Ezra, Chronicles, Job and Proverbs. Cognates: Aramaic *qəbēl* "in front of", Old Aramaic and Palmyrene *lqbl* "over against", Sabaean *qbl* "accept", Arabic قَبِلَ "accept, admit" and قِبَل "front, opposite", Ethiopic ቀበለ "go to meet, accept", Assyrian *qablu* "encounter" [verified: BDB s.v. קָבַל, BDB08631]. Klein derives the receiving senses of קבל I from "to be opposite" [verified]; "to cry out, complain" is a separate root, Klein's קבל II [verified]. Accepting is etymologically **facing-and-taking**. (Akkadian *qablu*: BDB's gloss "encounter"; I did not verify further senses such as "battle; middle" [from memory: not re-checked]. IPA [qablu].)
- **A homonym that a forge must know about.** Jastrow separates קָבַל I, Piel "to receive, accept; to take an obligation upon one's self; to contract, agree", from **קָבַל II, Qal "to feel oppressed; to cry; to complain, bring charges"** (m. Yadayim 4:6 *qōbəlīn ʾānū ʿălēkhem*, "we cry out against you"). BDB also notes "NH קָבַל accuse". Aramaic has a third root קְבַל III, "to be misty, dark" [verified: Jastrow s.vv. קָבַל I, II, קְבַל ², קְבַל III; BDB]. In rabbinic Hebrew the *Qal* form *qābal* (the shape of the petition's "qabal") means "to complain", not "to accept". The accepting verb is the Piel *qibbēl*.
- **Morphology.** *naʿăśeh* and *nišmāʿ* are both Qal imperfect 1cpl with the prefix *n-*. A pausal *nišmāʿ* is identical in letters (and, in pause, in vowels) to the Niphal perfect 3ms "it was heard / it was obeyed" (Klein lists נִשְׁמַע "was obeyed"). Context decides [grammar from memory: not re-checked against a grammar]. In Modern Hebrew *ma nišmaʿ?*, "what is heard?", is the everyday "how's it going?" [from memory: not re-checked]. This matters for the learnability of any coinage built on *nišmāʿ*.
- **Doctrinal family.** *naʿăśeh wə-nišmāʿ* (covenant answer) · *qabbālat ʿōl malkhūt šāmayim* (acceptance of the yoke of the kingdom of heaven, m. Ber 2:2) · *qabbālat ʿōl miṣwōt* (acceptance of the yoke of the commandments) · *qabbālāh* as transmission (m. Avot 1:1: Moses received, *qibbēl*, and handed on, *məsārāh*) · *qiyyəmū wə-qibbəlū* (renewal by consent, Est 9:27).

## 5. The unnamed region — the gap-shadows

What English speakers feel and cannot name in one word:
- Saying "yes, I'll take it" to a task before you have read it all, because you trust the person who asked, and knowing that the yes binds you.
- The "I do" that does not report a feeling but changes one's standing.
- The moment a request stops being someone else's and becomes yours.
- The "yes" in a protocol (an accepted task, a signed acknowledgement) that is both "I have heard you" and "I am now on the hook".

Near-misses, each failing:

| Candidate | Why it fails |
|---|---|
| accept | loses the hearing: one accepts a parcel without hearing anything; there is no answer-to-a-speaker structure |
| obey | loses the freedom and keeps the hierarchy; obeying names compliance with a command, not the taking-on of a commission |
| promise / pledge | loses the uptake: a promise can be volunteered with no one having asked; nothing has been heard |
| undertake | close, but bureaucratic; no hearing, no relationship; it also has an English homonym (funeral undertaker) |
| consent | a permission granted to another's act, not the taking-on of an act as one's own |
| Arabic *samiʿnā wa-aṭaʿnā* | a witness, not a substitute: same family (š-m-ʿ) and in documented contact with the Sinai tradition (archaeology/hebrew/uptake-convergence-notes.md) |
| Latin *Spondesne? Spondeo* | a witness: the binding answer, but legal and transactional; no hearing-root (archaeology/latin/suscipere.md) |

**Canon collision check (2026-11-10, main at eca7c99):**
- **shemme** (canon/core/shemme.md) holds receptive hearing as a pre-volitional state; the target asks for the volitional act. (Canon's own words: "The receptivity is pre-volitional. You did not decide to be moved.")
- **qorvance** (canon/worship-action/qorvance.md) holds the offerer's approach through what is brought; the target needs the receiver's answer.
- **yadahance** is ORDO's one return form (ordo/SPEC.md); it acknowledges a result, not the taking-on of a task.
- None of the three holds "I take this upon myself, in answer to you". The element-level grep of the leads is in archaeology/hebrew/uptake-convergence-notes.md §6.

## 6. Convergence-mapping

- **Existing convergence.** convergences/hearing-as-shaping.md houses š-m-ʿ as the shemme witness. This file adds no new witness there. It shows that the act-side of that convergence is carried in Hebrew/Aramaic by q-b-l, not by š-m-ʿ.
- **New pattern, not yet housed: "acceptance as a binding answer".** Witnesses and their independence status are tabled in archaeology/hebrew/uptake-convergence-notes.md §2. In brief:
  - Arabic: same family and in contact, so not independent.
  - Latin, Greek and Germanic: one IE family; partly in contact through Bible translation and calque.
  - Sanskrit: IE.
  - Japanese, Chinese and Yoruba: independent families.

Convergence cardinality (independent families attesting acceptance as an answer that binds, this tradition included): 4, namely Semitic, Indo-European, Sinitic and Japonic, with Niger-Congo as a fifth on the looser "accept + hear" shape (Yoruba gbàgbọ́, which means "believe"). This is a count, not a score. Sinitic and Japonic are unrelated by descent but in deep written contact: the earliest dictionary citation for the "consent" sense of Japanese *uketamawaru* is a reading-gloss on Chinese 唯. See the honest notes in archaeology/hebrew/uptake-convergence-notes.md.

## 8. Forge-targets identified — leads only

House note: this section departs from the template on purpose. This dig is excavation only, so there is no lemma form, no IPA of a coinage and no score. Leads are stated with their weaknesses.

- **Standing target:** wire-task-accept (forge_targets.json, status queued; realm feature "the act of binding acceptance — hearing that completes itself in pledged uptake").
- **Lead H1 — q-b-l (Piel *qibbēl*, Targumic *nəqabbēl*).**
  - Strengths: it is the tradition's own word for the act, the one that translates *nišmāʿ*, and it joins *ʿal*, "upon oneself", to make binding explicit.
  - Weaknesses:
    - The English ear hears "Kabbalah".
    - The rabbinic Qal *qābal* means "to complain, accuse" (Jastrow II), and Aramaic q-b-l III means "dark, misty".
    - In Hebrew it is a late Aramaic loan (BDB).
    - The petition's own reach, "qabalance", contains the English word "balance" (and "cabal").
    - The canon already leans heavily on Hebrew donors in this register (shemme, qorvance, qinance, teshuvance, yadahance).
- **Lead H2 — *naʿăśeh* (do-first).**
  - Strength: names the striking feature, commitment before full knowledge.
  - Weaknesses:
    - It names the doing, not the uptake.
    - The order is contested: Deuteronomy reverses it, Rashbam dissolves it by tense, Sforno makes hearing a precondition, and the min attacks it. (The Targum is neutral and follows each source's order.)
    - It carries the coercion strand (the mountain) and a polemical history.
- **Lead H3 — š-m-ʿ again.**
  - Strength: the target's own sketch (`hebrew/shema-uptake`).
  - Weaknesses:
    - The root is already spent on shemme. A second š-m-ʿ coinage would make the state/act distinction hang on a suffix alone.
    - *nišmāʿ* is everyday Modern Hebrew small talk.
- **Status:** queued. This file supplies one of the two archaeologies the sketched donor pair names; whether the Hebrew side should be š-m-ʿ or q-b-l is a question for Yu (see the return note).
