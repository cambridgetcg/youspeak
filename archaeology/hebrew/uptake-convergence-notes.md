## 1. What counts as a match

Three shapes, from weakest to strongest:

1. **hear → obey, as lexical polysemy.** One verb covers "hear" and "obey". This is cross-linguistically common: Sweetser (1990, *From Etymology to Pragmatics*, ch. 2) on Indo-European, and Evans & Wilkins (2000, *Language* 76) on Australian languages, describe hearing verbs extending to obeying and understanding [from memory: not re-checked]. **A match of this kind shows little that is special.**
2. **taking-up as undertaking.** A verb of receiving or lifting that also means "take on a task". Moderately specific.
3. **acceptance spoken as a binding answer to a speaker.** A first-person reply to a request or offer that creates an obligation. This is the shape the wire performative needs, and the one worth counting.

**Count, not score.**
- On shape 3 (binding answer): independent families attesting it are **Semitic, Indo-European (Roman stipulatio; Skt. pratijñā; Gk. homologeō in its native promissory sense), Sinitic (諾 / 應承) and Japonic (承る)**, which is 4. Niger-Congo adds a fifth only on the looser accept + hear compound (*gbàgbọ́*), whose sense is "believe". Sinitic and Japonic are independent by descent but in heavy written contact (see the uketamawaru row), so a stricter count that discounts contact gives 3 plus 1 with caveat.
- On shape 1 (hear ~ obey): Semitic, IE, Sinitic (聽, unverified), Niger-Congo (*gbọ́*, *sikia*). Shape 1 is typologically common and weak evidence on its own.

## 5. Leads for a forge — the accept meaning (no scores)

Each lead is a direction, not a coinage. Weaknesses first-class.

| Lead | What it would carry | Honest weaknesses |
|---|---|---|
| **A1 q-b-l** (Heb. *qibbēl*, Aram. *nəqabbēl*) | the tradition's own word for the act; renders *nišmāʿ* in Onkelos (Ex 24:7, Dt 5:24); *qibbēl ʿal* = bind oneself | "Kabbalah" dominates the English ear, and the canon itself uses Kabbalah/Kabbalistic in 24 files (§6). | **A2 spond-** (*Spondes? | **A4 recip-** | binding acceptance of what another entrusts (*fidei commissae*) | no hearing; passive-sounding in English (receive, recipient). Pairs naturally with A3, which raises the doctrine question of one accept or two |

| **A6 uketamawaru 承る** | receive-and-hear → consent (NKD sense 3), the closest single-word gloss of the realm feature | six morae; humble keigo encodes rank (the hearer below the commander); common as shop-counter politeness (承りました) [from memory], which may sound servile |

| **A8 gbàgbọ́** (gbà + gbọ́) | take + hear in one verb: the realm feature's two halves compounded | means *believe*, not *undertake*; a living tradition, so protocol consultation is required; labial-velar [ɡ͡b] and tones; possible mission-translation shaping (unverified) |

## 7. Contradictions with the repo found while digging

1. `labs/logos/forge/qabalance-gap.md:14` says "Hebrew qabal". The accepting verb is Piel *qibbēl*. BDB marks it a late Aramaic loan and notes "NH קָבַל accuse"; Jastrow's קָבַל II (Qal) = "to complain, bring charges" [verified].
2. `canon/core/shemme.md:36` says "the Arabic *samiʿan wa ṭāʿatan*", and `convergences/hearing-as-shaping.md:23` calls it "the Quranic *samʿan wa ṭāʿatan*". The Qur'anic text is the verbal *samiʿnā wa-aṭaʿnā* (2:285, 4:46, 5:7, 24:51). A full-text search finds no وطاعة anywhere in the Qur'an [verified: api.alquran.cloud search, quran-simple-clean].
3. `canon/core/shemme.md:19, :41, :48` and `convergences/hearing-as-shaping.md:21, :41, :47` give the Akkadian verb as "*šēmu*"; the signs 𒈠𒁇 appear at shemme.md:19 and hearing-as-shaping.md:21 [line numbers re-checked]. BDB gives "Assyrian šemǔ" [verified]. Wiktionary gives *šemûm / šemû*, spelled 𒊺𒈬𒌑 še-mu-u₂, logograms GIŠ.TUK / ŠE.GA, citing CAD [verified; CAD not opened]. The repo's signs 𒈠𒁇 are Unicode CUNEIFORM SIGN MA + BAR, which do not spell the verb [verified with Python's unicodedata].
4. `forge_targets.json:4294` names donor `hebrew/shema-uptake`; no archaeology file of that name exists. The nearest is now archaeology/hebrew/naaseh-venishma.md, which finds the act-word to be q-b-l rather than š-m-ʿ. This is an observation, not a correction.
