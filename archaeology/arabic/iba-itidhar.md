# ʾAbā and iʿtidhār — "they declined to bear it, and were afraid of it"

_The Hebrew dig found that ʾābāh, "be willing", is almost always negated, and that its Arabic cognate ʾabā simply means "refuse". This file opens the Qur'anic uses of that verb and finds the sharpest pair in the whole excavation. The same verb names Iblīs's refusal to bow, the Qur'an's archetype of rupture, and the refusal of the heavens, the earth and the mountains when the Trust (al-amāna) was offered to them: they "declined to bear it and were afraid of it", and nothing in the verse blames them. Instead it is the human who took it up who is called "very unjust, very ignorant". The difference between the two refusals lies in what was refused (a command, an offer) and in how (with arrogance, with fear of failing). The file also opens ʿudhr, the excuse, in the Tabuk passages, where the Qur'an separates true excuses that carry no blame from false ones that are refused. The dig is shallow: Qur'anic loci and one modern tafsir only._

## 1. The concept in compact form

**أَبَى ʾabā** (imperfect *yaʾbā*, verbal noun *ʾibāʾ*) means "reject, refuse; have an aversion to" [verified: en.wiktionary.org/wiki/أبى, Arabic, Etymology 1]. Wiktionary reports a theory that the root's first meaning was "want", that it came to be used almost only with a negator (the state of Hebrew ʾābāh), and that standard Arabic then dropped the negator; one of its usage examples is the idiom *šiʾta ʾam ʾabayta*, "whether you wanted or refused" [verified: same entry; the theory is reported there with Landberg, Lane, Nöldeke and Steingass in its reference list, none of which I opened]. The entry also records an alternative view that explains the non-standard "want" senses from a mutilated *baghā* "to desire" [verified: same entry]. BDB independently gives Arabic أَبَى as "refuse" beside Hebrew "be willing" [verified: BDB s.v. אָבָה, sefaria.org/api/words/אבה].

**عُذْر ʿudhr** is "excuse"; **عَذَرَ ʿadhara** "to excuse (someone)", with antonym *lāma*, "to blame"; **اِعْتَذَرَ iʿtadhara** (Form VIII) "to excuse oneself; to apologise to", each sense listed with the prepositions *ilā* / *ʿan* [verified: en.wiktionary.org/wiki/عذر and wiki/اعتذر]. Wiktionary's illustrative quotation for *iʿtadhara* is a 2017 news sentence about a patient who had volunteered for a head-transplant operation: *iʿtadhara ʾakhīran ʿan qarārihi, wa-tanaḥḥā jāniban li-yufassiḥa l-majāla ʾilā marīḍin ʾākhar*, which Wiktionary translates "he eventually withdrew his decision and stepped aside to make way for another patient" [verified: same entry]. The stepping aside is a second verb (*tanaḥḥā jāniban*); *iʿtadhara ʿan* itself is "withdraw from, excuse oneself from".

**Structural properties the forge must preserve, or consciously decline:**

1. **One verb, two opposite fates.** Iblīs: *ʾabā wa-stakbara wa-kāna mina l-kāfirīn*, "he refused and was arrogant and became one of the disbelievers" (2:34). The heavens, earth and mountains: *fa-abayna ʾan yaḥmilnahā wa-ʾašfaqna minhā*, "they declined to bear it and were afraid of it" (33:72). The verb does not decide whether a refusal breaks the bond. What decides it is what was refused, and how.
2. **Command against offer.** Iblīs refuses a command (*qulnā … sjudū*, "We said … prostrate"). The Trust is offered (*ʿaraḍnā*, "We presented"). Refusing a command is rebellion; declining an offer is not condemned.
3. **The decline from fear of failing is unblamed; the acceptance is followed by a judgment on the one who accepted.** 33:72 ends on the human who took the Trust up: *innahu kāna ẓalūman jahūlā*, "he was very unjust, very ignorant". Whether that clause blames the act of accepting, or describes the human's nature, is a question of exegesis this dig did not open. al-Muyassar glosses the decliners' fear as fear "that they would not perform it", the human's acceptance as taking it on "despite his weakness", and the judgment as injustice and ignorance "toward himself" (§3.1). If the clause does blame the act, the shape parallels the min's objection in b. Shabbat 88a ("first you ought to have listened; if you were able, you would have accepted"), in a different text and tradition (§3.1: a parallel, not dependence).
4. **The excuse is weighed, not sovereign.** In the Tabuk passages a true excuse (weakness, illness, poverty, with sincerity) carries "no blame" (9:91); a false one is refused: "Make no excuse; we will never believe you" (9:94). *ʿUdhr* is a plea before a judge, like Latin *excusatio* (archaeology/latin/excusare-recusare.md), not a bare "I do not want".

**Sibling distinctions.**
- **ʾabā** is refusal as an act of the will. It can be rebellion (Iblīs) or a fearful, honest decline (the heavens).
- **iʿtidhār** is refusal by giving an excuse. It asks to be judged.
- **warʿ** (canon's warame) is abstention from the doubtful as a disposition. It refuses things, not people.

## 3. Primary attestations, each read to the end of its passage

### 3.1 Q 33:72 — the Trust offered, declined and borne

> إِنَّا عَرَضْنَا ٱلْأَمَانَةَ عَلَى ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ وَٱلْجِبَالِ فَأَبَيْنَ أَن يَحْمِلْنَهَا وَأَشْفَقْنَ مِنْهَا وَحَمَلَهَا ٱلْإِنسَٰنُ ۖ إِنَّهُۥ كَانَ ظَلُومًۭا جَهُولًۭا
> "We offered the Trust to the heavens and the earth and the mountains, and they declined to bear it and were afraid of it; and the human bore it. He was very unjust, very ignorant." (translation mine; Sahih International has "Indeed, we offered the Trust … and they declined to bear it and feared it; but man [undertook to] bear it. Indeed, he was unjust and ignorant.")
> [verified: api.alquran.cloud/v1/ayah/33:72/editions/quran-uthmani,en.sahih,ar.muyassar]

al-Muyassar: "the Trust — which God entrusted to those under obligation: obeying the commands and avoiding the prohibitions — … they declined to bear it, and feared that they would not carry out its performance (*wa-khifna ʾan lā yaqumna bi-ʾadāʾihā*), and the human bore it and bound himself to it despite his weakness (*wa-ltazama bihā ʿalā ḍaʿfihi*); he was exceedingly unjust and ignorant toward himself" [verified: same endpoint, ar.muyassar; translation mine].

- **Relation:** kept. The decliners are not punished, rebuked or cast out in the verse or the gloss. The censure falls on the one who accepted.
- **Pairing with acceptance:** yes, inside the verse: *ʾabayna* against *ḥamalahā*, refusing against carrying. The accept-word here is a verb of bearing a load, like Latin *suscipere* "take up from below" (archaeology/latin/suscipere.md).
- **Caution.** The amāna has a large exegetical history (what the Trust is, whether the heavens could choose, why the human is blamed) that was not opened for this dig [from memory: not re-checked]. The structural reading above rests on the verse and one modern tafsir.
- **Parallel, not dependence:** the shape matches the min's critique in b. Shabbat 88a.9 (accepting a burden before knowing one can bear it is rash). I make no claim about contact between the two texts on this point.

### 3.2 Q 2:34, 20:116 — Iblīs refuses

> فَسَجَدُوٓا۟ إِلَّآ إِبْلِيسَ أَبَىٰ وَٱسْتَكْبَرَ وَكَانَ مِنَ ٱلْكَٰفِرِينَ
> "so they prostrated, except Iblīs: he refused and was arrogant and became one of the disbelievers" (2:34; translation mine)
> 20:116 ends at *ʾabā*: "except Iblīs; he refused".
> [verified: api.alquran.cloud, 2:34 and 20:116, quran-uthmani and en.sahih]

- **Relation:** broken. The refusal is of a command (*qulnā … sjudū*), joined to arrogance (*istakbara*), and ends in disbelief. Further consequences in other suras (expulsion) were not opened [from memory: not re-checked].
- **What a decline-word must not import:** this is the Qur'an's own paradigm refusal. A lemma on *ʾab-* would be heard against it by any reader of the Qur'an.

### 3.3 Q 9:90–94, 9:66 — excuses weighed

The setting is the Tabuk campaign, as al-Muyassar names it at 9:94 [verified].
- 9:90: *wa-jāʾa l-muʿadhdhirūna mina l-ʾaʿrābi li-yuʾdhana lahum*, "those of the Bedouin with excuses came to be given leave"; others "who lied to God and His Messenger sat at home", and a painful punishment will strike "those of them who disbelieved". al-Muyassar: some came and showed "their weakness and lack of ability to go out", others stayed back "without an excuse they showed" [verified].
- 9:91: *laysa ʿalā ḍ-ḍuʿafāʾi wa-lā ʿalā l-marḍā wa-lā ʿalā lladhīna lā yajidūna mā yunfiqūna ḥarajun ʾidhā naṣaḥū li-llāhi wa-rasūlihi*, "there is no blame on the weak, nor on the sick, nor on those who find nothing to spend, when they are sincere to God and His Messenger" [verified]. al-Muyassar calls them *ʾahl al-ʾaʿdhār*, "the people of excuses" [verified].
- 9:93: blame rests "only on those who ask your leave while they are rich" [verified].
- 9:94: *yaʿtadhirūna ʾilaykum … qul lā taʿtadhirū lan nuʾmina lakum*, "they will make excuses to you … say: make no excuse, we will never believe you" [verified].
- 9:66: *lā taʿtadhirū qad kafartum baʿda ʾīmānikum*, "make no excuse; you have disbelieved after your belief" [verified]. This verse is not about a declined summons: al-Muyassar ties it to the mocking words of 9:65 (*bi-hādhā l-maqāli lladhī stahzaʾtum bihi*) [verified]. It shows a refused excuse, not a refused task.
- 7:164: the warners of the Sabbath-breakers say they warn *maʿdhiratan ʾilā rabbikum*, "as an excuse before your Lord" (al-Muyassar: "so that we are excused (*li-nuʿdhar*) concerning them") [verified]. Here *maʿdhira* is not a decline but discharge of a duty.

- **Relation:** depends on the truth of the excuse. A true excuse with sincerity keeps the relation without blame; a false one breaks it ("we will never believe you").
- **Polite refusal not free:** the decline from a summons to campaign must be an excuse, asked as leave (*istiʾdhān*), and it is judged. There is no "I do not want" here.

## 4. Etymology and family

- **ʾ-b-y.** Arabic "refuse" against Hebrew "be willing" (BDB; Wiktionary). If the negation theory holds, Arabic's refusal verb is a fossilised "not want" [reported, not verified in a primary Semitist source].
- **ʿ-dh-r.** Wiktionary derives it from Proto-West-Semitic *ʕaḏr-, "help", with cognates Sabaean "excuse, pardon" and Hebrew עֶזְרָה *ʿezrāh*, "help" [verified: en.wiktionary.org/wiki/عذر; not checked in a primary source]. Its descendants spread by borrowing: Persian *ʿuzr*, Hindi *uzr*, Turkish *özür*, Swahili *udhuru* [verified: same entry]. Those are contact, not independent witnesses.
- **Doctrinal family.** *ʾibāʾ* (refusal) · *istikbār* (arrogance, Iblīs) · *ʾišfāq* (fearful care, the heavens) · *ḥaml* (bearing, the human) · *ʿudhr* / *iʿtidhār* (excuse) · *istiʾdhān* (asking leave) · *ḥaraj* (blame).

## 5. The unnamed region — the gap-shadows

- Declining something offered because you fear you could not do it justice, and being right to.
- The difference, carried by context alone in Arabic, between refusing what was commanded and declining what was offered.
- An excuse that is true and sincere, and therefore leaves no blame.

Near-misses:

| Candidate | Why it fails |
|---|---|
| *ʾabā* | the verb is neutral between Iblīs and the heavens; the bond-keeping sense comes from 33:72's context, not the word |
| *iʿtidhār* | a plea that must be judged true; also the everyday word for "apology" |
| *warʿ* (warame) | abstention from the doubtful, not a decline spoken to a person |

**Canon collision check of the region:** warame (canon/core/warame.md, the Sufi station of scrupulous abstention) is the nearest Arabic-donor canon word. Its gap is abstention from the doubtful as a disposition; it has no addressee. No canon word holds the declined offer of 33:72.

## 6. Convergence-mapping

Semitic share of the cross-family picture (the other families are in the sibling files' §6):
1. **Decline offered as an equal option:** 33:72 frames the Trust as offered (*ʿaraḍnā*).
2. **A refusal that leaves the relation whole:** 33:72 (the heavens unblamed); 9:91 (the true excuse, "no blame").
3. **Decline as excuse:** *iʿtidhār ʿan* (excuse oneself from = decline). Compare Latin *excusare* (< *causa*), Chinese 辭 (pleading statement; archaeology/chinese/ci-rang-declining.md), Japanese *kotowaru* (giving reasons → declining; archaeology/japanese/kotowaru-enryo.md).
4. **The refusal that ruptures:** Iblīs (2:34); false excuses (9:94).

Independence: same family as the Hebrew dig, and in contact with it through the covenant narrative. Counted with Hebrew as one Semitic witness.

## 8. Forge-targets identified — leads only

No lemma, no coinage, no score.

- **Lead A-D1 — ʾibāʾ / ʾabā.**
  - Carries: the bare act of refusing, with the one Qur'anic case where a refusal of something offered is left unblamed (33:72).
  - Weaknesses: it is Iblīs's verb (2:34, 20:116), the Qur'an's archetype of rupture; living scripture (§0); in Hebrew the same root means "be willing", a contranym across the family; for the English ear *aba* sits near *abba* (§9).
- **Lead A-D2 — ʿudhr / iʿtidhār.**
  - Carries: decline as giving an account; the true excuse that leaves no blame (9:91); *iʿtadhara ʿan*, "withdraw from, excuse oneself from" (in Wiktionary's example the stepping aside for another is a separate verb, §1).
  - Weaknesses: an excuse asks to be judged, so it is not sovereign; the everyday sense is "apology"; the Qur'anic uses are mostly of false excuses (9:66, 9:94); widely borrowed, so not distinctive.
- **Lead A-D3 — the 33:72 shape: offered (*ʿaraḍnā*), declined (*fa-abayna*), feared (*ʾašfaqna*).**
  - Carries: the decline from fear of failing, set beside an acceptance followed by a judgment on the one who accepted (whether that judgment falls on the act of accepting is exegesis not opened here): the clearest scriptural case in this dig of an offer declined without blame.
  - Weaknesses: a verse, not a lexeme; the amāna's exegesis was not opened; taking a sacred verse as a template for a protocol performative needs custodial care and must be said aloud.
