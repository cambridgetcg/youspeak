# ancient-thought · reference shelf

Two dossiers on ancient texts and what later ages made of them, kept as reference: an understanding of how people thought in ancient times. Both follow the old material into its later reception (medieval and early-modern grimoires, 19th- and 20th-century occultism, and in tree-of-life.md modern art and film), and tree-of-life.md §9 also summarises modern biology's tree and web of life. That section is the history of an image; for the science itself, the cited papers are the source, not this shelf. The dossiers are not donor-tradition files and hold no candidate morphemes. They are not grounds, proof or authority for any doctrine, canon entry or standard, and they are not evidence of how anything works.

> _"… they are for reference only. Understanding always update truth. It is an understanding of how people think in ancient times."_ — Yu, 2026-10-31

## What is here

| File | What it covers |
|---|---|
| [lost-books.md](lost-books.md) | 燈下藏書室. The books the Bible names but did not keep: lost source-books cited in the Hebrew Bible, outside books quoted in the New Testament, the "books with power" inside scripture, later grimoires attributed to Solomon or Moses, and where each church drew the canon line. |
| [tree-of-life.md](tree-of-life.md) | 生命樹導賞. The tree of life from Eden to Asgard, with its shadows: every biblical occurrence, the neighbouring Near Eastern, Egyptian, Iranian and Greek trees, the Jewish, Christian and Kabbalistic readings, Norse, Indian, Buddhist, Chinese, Islamic, Gnostic and Manichaean trees, the scientific tree and web, and the "opposite" trees. |

Both are written in Cantonese. Chinese Bible quotations marked (和合本) are the Chinese Union Version as printed in its new-punctuation edition (新標點和合本, BibleGateway's CUVMPT); the older CUV text differs in some characters (for example 作/做, 惟/唯), so check quotations against CUVMPT. (意譯) marks the researcher's own rendering from a checked translation, not a published one. (原文) marks a quotation left in its original language (Classical Chinese, Latin, Italian, English and others); (暫譯) marks a Chinese title the researchers coined because no common one exists; (推論) marks the researchers' own inference.

## Evidence tags

Most claims carry a tag saying what kind of support they have. A few sentences carry none: some continue the tagged paragraph above them, others are the researchers' own linking or summary (for example lost-books.md §2 on Jude, tree-of-life.md §8 on the Gnostics).

| Tag | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 【經文】 | The primary source itself says so: in lost-books.md the Bible or the ancient book in question; in tree-of-life.md also modern primary sources such as Darwin's notebooks and scientific papers. It records what the source says, not that what it says happened. |
| 【共識】 | Broad scholarly consensus. |
| 【爭議】 | Disputed among scholars. |
| 【傳說】 | Legend or later tradition. |
| 【偽託】 | Pseudepigraphal: a work falsely attributed to an earlier figure, or forged. Used in lost-books.md. |
| 【現代】 | A modern invention or modern framing. Used in tree-of-life.md. |

tree-of-life.md also grades claims that one tradition shaped another:

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 有文獻證明 | Documented: a text or object shows the contact, such as a rewritten verse, a loanword, or a tablet copied locally. Contact shown is not by itself proof of borrowing (tree-of-life.md §3). |
| 可能 | Possible. |
| 只係平行 | Parallel only: alike, with no evidence that one came from the other. |
| 臆測 | Speculation. |

Cross-cultural likeness is read as parallel unless a document shows the line of descent (tree-of-life.md §7).

## How far they reach

They record what people believed and how belief was kept, copied, forged and argued over, including places where a later book or diagram was taken for an older one (lost-books.md §1.2; tree-of-life.md §6, §12).

The path survey in `pipeline/paths.py` reads every subdirectory of `archaeology/` as a donor tradition (`pipeline/paths.py:187-199`), so this shelf shows up in its output three times: as "0 canon (forge-ready)" in `--list-archaeology` (`pipeline/paths.py:876`), as a two-file entry in `--gaps`, a list the script calls "the cathedral's explicit forge-queue" (`pipeline/paths.py:818-819`), and in the summary's forge-ready count (`pipeline/paths.py:305`). All three come from the directory listing. Nothing on this shelf is waiting to be forged.

## Provenance

- Researched and written 2026-10-27 by a group of research agents. Each topic cluster was checked against its sources, then a separate critic pass flagged errors and overclaims, and those corrections were folded in before the text was final.
- All of those agents were one model family, so their agreement does not count as many independent witnesses: shared training means shared blind spots. Independence among such agents "is real but bounded" (KINGDOM-STANDARDS/specs/KS-000-process.md:147-149).
- Shelved here 2026-10-31. Each file gained a short header. The body is unchanged except for the in-place corrections listed, with what the text said before, in the file's closing "Register note (2026-10-31)". That note also qualifies a few tags and passages without changing them.
- At shelving, a small sample of facts was re-checked against outside sources. That check and the reviews of the shelving led to the corrections and qualifications in those Register notes. One claim, that Cordovero counted 22 channels (tree-of-life.md §6), could not be confirmed either way and stands as the dossier has it. Nor could the claim that Kircher was the first to letter the 22 paths, which tree-of-life.md tags three different ways (§6, §11, §12): a printed sefirot tree by Philippe d'Aquin (Paris, 1625) came before his, and whether its paths carry letters was not checked (see that file's Register note). A sample is not a re-verification.
- Both files remain open to correction, and each ends its body with a list of open questions (lost-books.md §9, tree-of-life.md §14). When later understanding corrects something, the cathedral's habit is to correct by addition, recording the change beside the text rather than rewriting silently (see [../../ONTOLOGY.md](../../ONTOLOGY.md) §1; for example the "Correction (third wave)" note in [../../ordo/SPEC.md](../../ordo/SPEC.md)), so the earlier reading stays visible.

The second 念之基 line, 「留下過，唔等於而家仍然真。」 (gloss, not Yu's wording: having been recorded is not being true now), holds for these dossiers too. [../../ONTOLOGY.md](../../ONTOLOGY.md) quotes all three lines in §1 and reads some of this material in §8.
