# Clusivity survey — is "you" visible inside the inclusive "we"?

Companion notes to archaeology/tok-pisin/pronouns-clusivity-and-number.md, from the 2026-11-10
language pass on the ankyrance donors. Written by Claude (Opus 5.5), subagent; re-checked by a
second reader (see the Verification section at the end).

**The question.** The ankyrance chapter's Law of the Visible Constituents (grammars/ankyrance/
manifesto.md, lines 61–66) says a union-form must keep both persons legible, "as 我 survives inside
我哋". The law needs languages whose inclusive "we" visibly contains "you" and "I". For each language
below: the forms, IPA, a source, and a plain verdict on four grades:

- **compositional** — the inclusive is built from the language's own words or markers for "you" and "I";
- **partial** — "you" (or "I") is visible, the other only as a shared plural ending;
- **I-only** — the first person survives inside the plural, "you" does not;
- **opaque** — the inclusive is its own root or prefix, built from neither.

## 1. Quechua — ñuqanchik / ñuqayku

- Forms: *ñuqa* "I"; *ñuqanchik* "we (inclusive)"; *ñuqayku* "we (exclusive)"; *qam* "you", *qamkuna*
  "you (pl.)" [verified: Wikipedia "Southern Quechua", pronoun table,
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Quechua]. Dialect spellings: Ayacucho *ñuqanchik*, Cusco
  *nuqanchis* [verified: same, dialect table].
- Possessive suffixes: *-y* "my", *-yki* "your", *-n* "his/her", *-nchik* "our (incl.)", *-y-ku* "our
  (excl.)", *-yki-chik* "your (pl.)" (*wasinchik* "our house (incl.)", *wasiyku* "our house (excl.)")
  [verified: same, possessive table, which itself segments *-y-ku*].
- Verdict: **I-only.** Both "we" pronouns are built on *ñuqa* "I"; the second person (*qam*, *-yki*)
  appears in neither. It is the *exclusive* suffix that is segmentable (*-y* "my" + *-ku*); the
  inclusive *-nchik* is its own marker. Whether *-nchik* is historically analysable was not checked.
- Complication: Imbabura (Ecuadorian) Quechua has **no** inclusive/exclusive opposition [verified: WALS
  39A, "Quechua (Imbabura)": "No inclusive/exclusive", https://wals.info/feature/39A.tab]. "Quechua"
  is a family, not one system.
- History: the first European description of clusivity anywhere is Domingo de Santo Tomás's Quechua
  grammar of 1560 [verified: Cysouw, WALS ch. 39, https://wals.info/chapter/39].
- IPA: Quechua penultimate stress and lowering of /u/ near /q/ (giving [ɲoˈqant͡ʃɪk]) are [from memory:
  not re-checked].

## 2. Cantonese — 我哋

- Form: 我哋 *ngo5 dei6* "we, us" [verified: Wiktionary "我哋", https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/我哋];
  哋 *dei6* is the "plural marker for pronouns" (我哋, 你哋 *nei5 dei6* "you pl.", 佢哋 *keoi5 dei6*
  "they"), "possibly from" 等 [verified: Wiktionary "哋"].
- Clusivity: "There is no inclusive/exclusive 'we' in Cantonese" [verified: APiCS datapoint 20-15,
  Chinese Pidgin English, comment by Michelle Li and Stephen Matthews,
  https://apics-online.info/valuesets/20-15].
- Verdict: **not a clusivity form; I-only.** 我 "I" survives inside 我哋; 你 "you" is not in it, and
  the same word serves whether or not the hearer is included.
- IPA: tone letters for Jyutping 5 (˩˧) and 6 (˨) [from memory: not re-checked].

## 3. Mandarin — 咱們 / 我們

- 咱們 *zánmen*: "inclusive we; us"; "Unlike 我們, which can either include or exclude the person
  spoken to, 咱們 always includes the person spoken to"; "in practice … used primarily only in Northern
  China" and "tends to convey a sense of intimacy" [verified: Wiktionary "咱們", usage notes].
- 我們 *wǒmen*: "can either include or exclude the person being spoken to. In Northern China, 我們 is
  generally used in the exclusive sense while 咱們 is used for the inclusive sense" [verified:
  Wiktionary "我們", usage notes]. WALS glosses *wŏmen* as the exclusive and *zámen* "we, I and you"
  [verified: WALS ch. 39, introduction] — a northern-standard description that the usage notes qualify.
- Etymology of 咱: "Fusion of 自家 'self' (Song) > Modern Mandarin zá (Lü, 1984). Fusion with 們
  produces the form with a nasal coda (Yuan) … zán (Norman, 1988)"; 咱 is also literary or dialectal
  "I" [verified: Wiktionary "咱", Etymology 1; Lü 1984 and Norman 1988 not opened].
- Origin of the distinction: northern dialects "acquired" it [verified: WALS ch. 39, §2]; use of 咱们
  "may be from Manchu influence" [verified: Wikipedia "Clusivity" table note, citing Matthews 2010:
  760; Matthews not opened]. Mei Tsu-lin's Altaic-contact account (1988) is [from memory: not
  re-checked].
- Verdict: **opaque.** Neither 我 nor 你 is inside 咱; 咱 is a contraction of a word for "self". 我們 is
  I + plural.
- Southern Min 咱 *lán* "we (incl.)" has a different etymology (from 儂 "person; I") [verified:
  Wiktionary "咱", Etymology 3] — same character, separate history.

## 4. Malay — kita / kami

- Proto-Austronesian *i-(k)ita "1PL.INCL" and *i-(k)ami "1PL.EXCL": "they are lexemes sui generis and
  are not derived from one another nor from 1SG or 2SG pronouns" (PMP 1SG *i-aku, 2SG *i-kahu)
  [verified: Adelaar & Hajek, "Free personal pronouns", ch. 52 of *The Oxford Guide to the
  Malayo-Polynesian Languages of Southeast Asia*, preprint p. 3,
  https://rest.mars-prod.its.unimelb.edu.au/server/api/core/bitstreams/c41b7c9d-149f-4cdb-803a-715211f8479e/content].
- Standard Indonesian: 1SG *aku* (polite *saya*), 2SG *(əŋkaw)*, *kamu*, 1PL *kita* (incl.), *kami*
  (excl.) [verified: same, Table 52.5, Standard Indonesian column, citing Sneddon 2010].
- Complication: "Colloquial Indonesian kita, Jakarta Malay kitɛ and, historically, Vehicular Malay
  *kita, all refer to any first person, with no number or inclusive/exclusive distinction" [verified:
  same, preprint p. 7]. APiCS: Ambon Malay, Singapore Bazaar Malay and Sri Lankan Malay lost the
  distinction [verified: APiCS ch. 15].
- A real secondary composition: where *kita* narrowed to "you and I", some languages rebuilt an
  inclusive plural by adding a second-person clitic — Iraya *tamu* < *kita + *-mu "2PL.GEN" — or a
  quantifier — Sinama Bajau *kitabi* < *kita + *qabis "all" [verified: same, preprint p. 13, citing Reid
  2016: 155]. IPA [ˈtamu], [kiˈtabi] [from memory: not re-checked].
- Verdict: **opaque** for Malay *kita*. Iraya *tamu* is **partial**: an opaque "you-and-I" plus a
  visible "you (pl.)".

## 5. Tamil — nām / nāṅkaḷ

- நாம் *nām* "we (inclusive)", inherited from Proto-Dravidian *ñām "we (inclusive)"; also "by
  extension, honorific you" and archaic plural/honorific of "I" [verified: Wiktionary "நாம்",
  https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/நாம்].
- நாங்கள் *nāṅkaḷ* "we (exclusive)", "from நான் 'I' + -கள் plural marker"; in Jaffna also used as a
  synonym of inclusive நாம் [verified: Wiktionary "நாங்கள்", citing Cre-A 1992 for the Jaffna use].
- நீ *nī* "you" < PD *nīn [verified: Wiktionary "நீ"].
- APiCS: "Tamil has the distinction" (and Sri Lankan Malay did not adopt it from Tamil) [verified:
  APiCS ch. 15]. Krishnamurti, *The Dravidian Languages* (2003), on PD *ñām / *yām: [from memory: not
  re-checked].
- Verdict: **opaque** inclusive. Tamil reverses the Quechua pattern: here it is the *exclusive* that is
  "I + plural".

## 6. Hawaiian — kāua, māua, kākou, mākou

- *kāua* "first person dual inclusive: we (you and I only)", from *kā-* (inclusive prefix) + *lua*
  "two"; *māua* "dual exclusive: he/she and I, but not you", from Proto-Polynesian *ma-ua; *kākou*
  "plural inclusive", from *kā-* + *kolu* "three"; *mākou* "plural exclusive", from *mā-* + *kolu*
  [verified: Wiktionary "kāua", "māua", "kākou", "mākou"; uncited entries]. APiCS lists Hawaiian
  *kakou*/*makou* [verified: APiCS ch. 15, table 1].
- *au* "I" and *ʻoe* "you": [from memory: not re-checked]. Pukui & Elbert's dictionary (wehewehe.org)
  refused the request (HTTP 403); Elbert & Pukui, *Hawaiian Grammar* (1979), not opened.
- Verdict: **opaque for person.** The inclusive is marked by *kā-*, which is neither "I" nor "you".
  The numerals are not plainly visible either. Wiktionary derives *kāua* from *kā-* + *lua* "two" and
  *kākou* from *kā-* + *kolu* "three", each "with a similar loss of *l*", so *-ua* and *-kou* are only
  historically recoverable. That the plurals continue old trials ("three") is Wiktionary's etymology,
  not checked against POLLEX. Hawaiian and Malay are one family (Austronesian), so they are not two
  independent witnesses. That Hawaiian *kā-* and *mā-* continue PAn *kita and *kami is [from memory: not
  re-checked].

## 7. Ojibwe — giinawind / niinawind

- *giinawind* "we, us [inclusive]" and *niinawind* "we, us [exclusive]"; example "Giinawind
  giga-ozhiga'igemin. We'll put in the taps." [verified: Ojibwe People's Dictionary,
  https://ojibwe.lib.umn.edu/main-entry/giinawind-pron-per and /niinawind-pron-per].
- Paradigm: 1s *niin* "I", 2s *giin* "you (sg.)", 3s *wiin*, 1p *niinawind* "we (excluding you)",
  **21** *giinawind* "we (including you)", 2p *giinawaa*, 3p *wiinawaa* [verified: UW–Madison Ojibwe
  grammar, personal pronouns,
  https://ojibwegrammar.langsci.wisc.edu/Grammar/HTMLParadigms/prpersonal.htm]. The grammar's own
  label for the inclusive, "21", is second-plus-first.
- Reading of the paradigm (mine, from the table above): *giin-awind* = the stem of *giin* "you" + the
  first-plural ending *-awind* shared with *niin-awind*. The verb shows the same build: *gi-…-min*
  (second-person prefix + first-plural suffix) in *giga-ozhiga'igemin*.
- Related: Plains Cree *kiyānaw* / *niyanān* [verified: Wikipedia "Clusivity" table; uncited]; Michif
  *kiyanaan* / *niiyanaan*, retained from Cree [verified: APiCS ch. 15, table 3]; Maliseet-Passamaquoddy
  verbs *k-tíhin-èn* (incl.) / *n-tíhin-èn* (excl.) [verified: Wikipedia "Clusivity"].
- Verdict: **partial.** "You" is fully visible; "I" is present only as first-person-plural agreement,
  not as *niin*. The closest of the eight to the law. The same build is reported for Plains Cree ("The
  inclusive form is derived from the second-person singular pronoun"), for Moose Cree, and for Shawnee
  *kiilawe* ("morphologically derived from the second-person pronoun *kiila*") [verified: Wikipedia
  "Clusivity", table notes; uncited there]. That it holds for the whole Algonquian family is
  [from memory: not re-checked]. Ojibwe, Cree, Michif, Shawnee and Maliseet-Passamaquoddy are all
  Algonquian (Michif by retention from Cree), so they count as one witness, not five.
- IPA: Ojibwe ⟨g⟩, ⟨d⟩ are lenis stops; ⟨ii⟩ long [iː] [from memory: not re-checked].

## 8. Cherokee — ini- / idi- against osdi- / oji-

- Set A pronominal prefixes: 1sg *ji-/g-*; 1 dual inclusive *ini-/in-*, 1 dual exclusive *osdi-/osd-*;
  1 plural inclusive *idi-/id-*, 1 plural exclusive *oji-/oj-*; 2sg *hi-/h-*, 2 dual *sdi-/sd-*, 2 plural
  *iji-/ij-*; 3pl *ani-/an-*. The article adds: "the exclusive forms are generated by taking the
  corresponding 2nd person form and adding an 'O'" [verified: Wikipedia "Cherokee grammar", Set I (A)
  table, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherokee_grammar; the table and the remark are uncited].
- Free pronoun ᎠᏴ *ayv* "I, me; we, us" [verified: Wiktionary "ᎠᏴ"]; "Free pronouns do not
  distinguish clusivity" [verified: Wikipedia "Clusivity", Cherokee row; uncited].
- Montgomery-Anderson, *Cherokee Reference Grammar* (University of Oklahoma Press, 2015) and Feeling
  & Pulte (1975): [from memory: not re-checked]. A Cherokee Nation grammar handout found by search could
  not be downloaded (connection refused).
- Verdict: **opaque** inclusive: *ini-*, *idi-* contain neither *hi-* "you" nor *ji-/g-* "I". If the
  Wikipedia remark holds, Cherokee does the reverse of the law's expectation: its *exclusive* carries a
  second-person-shaped piece (*o-sdi*, *o-ji* beside *sdi-*, *iji-*). That remark is unverified and
  should not be repeated without the grammar.
- IPA: Cherokee ⟨d⟩ = unaspirated [t]~[d], ⟨j⟩ = [tʃ]~[dʒ], ⟨v⟩ = nasal schwa [from memory: not
  re-checked].

## 9. Contact languages and other comparisons

- **Tok Pisin** *yumi* < "you (and) me", *mipela* < "me fellow"; Bislama *yumi*/*mifala*; Australian
  Kriol *yunmi*, *minyu* (incl.) / *minbala*, *mindubala* (excl.); Norf'k dual inclusive *himii/hamii*
  [verified: APiCS ch. 15, https://apics-online.info/parameters/15.chapter.html]. Verdict:
  **compositional** for Tok Pisin, Bislama and Kriol. Each builds a substrate category from lexifier
  words, and they count as one witness, sharing English and a Pacific lineage. Two cases do not fit
  that verdict:
  - **Norf'k.** *himii/hamii* shows only *mii* "me" (my reading of the form; APiCS gives no etymology
    for *hi-/ha-*). APiCS places Norf'k among distinctions "not (or not clearly) derived from a
    substrate", limits the distinction to the dual, and doubts that the exclusive is grammaticalized
    [verified: APiCS ch. 15, §4].
  - **Chinese Pidgin English.** APiCS codes it "No inclusive/exclusive distinction". Its rendering of
    English "we" "variously as my, my long you or two man" includes one phrase, *my long you* "me with
    you", that shows both persons, but it is no pronoun [verified: APiCS 20-15].
  APiCS's §4 also gives Tayo (French-lexified, New Caledonia) *nu n de ave twa* "[we.two with you]" for
  the dual inclusive, against *nu n de sa twa* "[we.two without you]". There "you" is visible inside a
  phrase, and APiCS is unsure it is grammaticalized [verified: APiCS ch. 15, §4]. Tok Pisin's own
  substrate, Tolai, has an **opaque** inclusive (*dor, datal, dat*) [verified: Mosel 1980, p. 61,
  http://sealang.net/archives/pl/pdf/PL-B73.pdf].
- **Lakota** *uŋ(k)-* "I and thou" (dual, subject-only), plural *uŋ(k)-…-pi* with no clusivity
  [verified: Riggs 1893 §§13, 18; Wikipedia "Lakota language" citing Rood & Taylor 1996; Rood 2005 on
  the SIOUAN list]. *mitákuye* is "my relative", not a "we" [verified: Riggs 1893 §22; Riggs 1890 p.
  455]. Verdict: **opaque**. Rood on the same list calls the plural *unyanpi* "neither exclusive nor
  inclusive -- it is 'I and others'" [verified: SIOUAN list, 14 Dec 2005, post 006022, quoting Rood].
- **Pirahã**: no specialised "we"; "the only way to express a 'we'-like meaning is by a conjunction of
  the pronouns for 'I' and 'you'": *ti gíxai pí-o ahápií* "You and I will go" [verified: WALS ch. 39,
  ex. (1), citing Everett 1986: 281]. Verdict: **compositional by conjunction**, because there is no
  "we" word at all. IPA of *gíxai* with ⟨x⟩ as a glottal stop [ɡíʔai]: [from memory: not re-checked].

## 10. What the survey says about the Law of Visible Constituents

1. None of the eight languages surveyed here has an inclusive built from its own "you" and "I".
   Several are opaque (Mandarin 咱們, Malay *kita*, Tamil *nām*, Hawaiian *kāua/kākou*, Cherokee *ini-/
   idi-*), as expected. Quechua keeps only "I". Cantonese makes no distinction at all. Ojibwe is the
   one partial case: "you" visible, "I" only as a plural ending.
2. Fully visible you-plus-I inclusives, in what was opened, come from **contact languages** (Tok Pisin
   and its English-lexified relatives) and from **phrase-level conjunction** in a language without "we"
   (Pirahã). The transparency of *yumi* is a product of pidginisation, not a widespread design of human
   grammars.
3. 我哋 cannot carry the law: it keeps one person, not two, and does not mark inclusion.
4. The law can still be YOUSPEAK doctrine — a design choice — but its honest grounding is "Tok Pisin
   *yumi* (one contact cluster) and, partly, the Algonquian 21 forms", not "many languages".
5. Independence. For counting, the eight languages are fewer witnesses than they look. Mandarin and
   Cantonese are one family (Sinitic). Malay and Hawaiian are one family (Austronesian), and Tolai, Tok
   Pisin's substrate, belongs to the same family. The Algonquian forms count as one. For Malay, the
   opaque *kita* is the inherited PAn form (§4). That the Hawaiian prefixes continue the same pair is
   [from memory: not re-checked].
