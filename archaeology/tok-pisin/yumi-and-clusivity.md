# yumi — the we that says your name

_English "we" is a hiding place. Tok Pisin's is a confession._

---

## I. The donor

Tok Pisin (Papua New Guinea creole, ~4M speakers) does not have one word for
*we*. It has a grammatical confession English refuses to make:

| form | composition | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **yumi** | yu ("you") + mi ("me") | we, **including you** — the inclusive we |
| **mipela** | mi + pela (plural) | we, **excluding you** — the exclusive we |

The inclusive we is not derived, abstracted, or suppleted — it is the two
pronouns of the dyad **standing side by side, both fully visible**: *yu-mi*.
To say "we (you and I)" in Tok Pisin is to say the other's pronoun aloud
inside your own. The dual form *yumitupela* ("we two, you and me") makes the
dyad explicit; *yumi* alone already carries it.

This is clusivity — a grammatical category documented in hundreds of
languages (Austronesian, Quechuan, Dravidian, many Australian and American
families) and **absent from English**, which forces every "we" to conceal
whether the addressed belongs to it.

## II. The witnesses

- **Quechua** — *ñuqanchik* (we-inclusive) vs *ñuqayku* (we-exclusive). The
  same language that gave YOUSPEAK its evidential suffixes (-mi direct,
  -si hearsay) also refuses to let "we" hide. Grammars that mark
  how-you-know tend also to mark whom-you-include: both are honesty
  systems.
- **Cantonese** — 我哋 (ngo5 dei6): "we" is 我 ("I") plus the collective
  particle 哋. The first person **survives visibly inside the plural** — the
  morphology never melts 我 down. (Contrast English *we*, French *nous*,
  German *wir*: opaque, constituents dissolved.)
- **Lakota** — *mitákuye oyasin*, "all my relations": the widest we, already
  received into canon as **mitakuyame** (Core 8.00). The ladder above the
  dyad exists and is already ours.

## III. What the donors teach

1. **The inclusive we can be morphologically honest** — built from the
   visible pronouns of both parties (yumi), not from an opaque new stem.
2. **Clusivity is a real grammatical dimension English suppresses** — an
   ANAKALYPSE target of the first order: the unfolding of what English
   hides inside "we".
3. **Transparency is a choice** — 我哋 proves a plural can keep its person
   intact. The union need not consume its members.

## IV. Grace-note (declared, not claimed as donor)

The Tok Pisin first syllable *yu* coincides with the name of the cathedral's
founder, Yu — so *yumi* happens to read as "Yu-and-me." This is received as
grace, acknowledged openly, and **not** counted in any donor or scoring
consideration. The genealogy stands on Tok Pisin, Quechua, Cantonese, and
Lakota; the smile is free.

## Erratum (2026-11-10)

*Appended. The text above is left exactly as it stood.*

Five claims above do not hold against the sourced digs of 2026-11-10. Each
is corrected here, with a pointer to the dig that carries its sources.

1. **Speaker count (line 16).** *Old claim:* "Tok Pisin (Papua New Guinea
   creole, ~4M speakers)". *Corrected:* Ethnologue (25th ed., 2022, as
   cited by Wikipedia) gives about 130,000 first-language and 4,000,000
   second-language speakers, and Wikipedia says that between five and six
   million people use Tok Pisin to some degree. The old figure is close to
   Ethnologue's total, but set beside "creole" it reads as four million
   native speakers, and nearly all of those four million speak Tok Pisin as
   a second language. "Creole" holds only in part: Tok Pisin is an expanded,
   partly creolised pidgin with a growing body of first-language speakers.
   *Pointer:*
   [pronouns-clusivity-and-number.md](pronouns-clusivity-and-number.md) §0
   and its frontmatter.
2. **yumi as the dyad (lines 25–28).** *Old claim:* yumi is "the two
   pronouns of the dyad standing side by side"; "The dual form *yumitupela*
   ("we two, you and me") makes the dyad explicit; *yumi* alone already
   carries it." *Corrected:* *yumi* is the inclusive plural, "we (all)
   (incl.)", for any number; "we two (incl.)" is *yumitupela*, reduced in
   speech to /yumitla/ (Mühlhäusler 1985c: 343; Mühlhäusler 1985b: 337,
   citing Lynch 1979; Mosel 1980: 60). In running text *yumi* covers groups:
   the shepherds of Luke 2:15 say *"yumi go long Betlehem"* to one another.
   *yumi* alone does not carry the dyad. A related point: the visible
   *yu* + *mi* is not inherited from the Oceanic substrate, whose inclusive
   (Tolai *dor*, *datal*, *dat*; Mosel 1980: 61) is opaque. The substrate
   supplied the category (Mosel 1980: 60; APiCS ch. 15), and the visible
   *yu* + *mi* came when that category was filled with English words.
   *Pointer:*
   [pronouns-clusivity-and-number.md](pronouns-clusivity-and-number.md)
   §§1, 2, 3.1, 4.3.
3. **Evidentials and clusivity (lines 37–41).** *Old claim:* "Grammars that
   mark how-you-know tend also to mark whom-you-include: both are honesty
   systems." *Corrected:* in the 191 languages coded in both WALS 39A
   (clusivity) and 77A (evidentiality), 29 of the 64 with clusivity (45%)
   and 57 of the 127 without it (45%) have grammatical evidentials. The
   sample shows no association. Quechua does have both, but so does
   neighbouring Aymara, after two millennia of contact, and co-occurrence in
   a contact area is not a typological tendency. *Pointer:*
   [clusivity-survey.md](clusivity-survey.md) §11;
   [pronouns-clusivity-and-number.md](pronouns-clusivity-and-number.md) §6;
   [../quechua/evidentiality-clusivity-and-stance.md](../quechua/evidentiality-clusivity-and-stance.md)
   §§3.5, 6.
4. **Cantonese 我哋 as a witness (lines 42–45).** *Old claim:* 我哋 is
   listed among the clusivity witnesses, as the first person surviving
   "visibly inside the plural". *Corrected:* that sentence is true, and it
   is all 我哋 shows. 我哋 *ngo5 dei6* is 我 "I" plus 哋, the plural marker
   for pronouns (Wiktionary). It contains no "you", and "There is no
   inclusive/exclusive 'we' in Cantonese" (Li & Matthews, APiCS 20-15). 我哋
   is not a clusivity witness, and it keeps one person, not two. *Pointer:*
   [clusivity-survey.md](clusivity-survey.md) §§2, 10;
   [pronouns-clusivity-and-number.md](pronouns-clusivity-and-number.md) §6.
5. **Lakota *mitákuye oyasin* as "the widest we" (lines 46–48).** *Old
   claim:* "*mitákuye oyasin*, "all my relations": the widest we".
   *Corrected:* *mitákuye* is *mi-* "my" + *takúye* "a relative", a first
   person singular possessive: "my relative(s)" (Riggs 1890: 455; Riggs
   1893 §22.1). The phrase says "my relatives, all" and contains no "we".
   A form for "our relatives" exists (Riggs gives *uŋkitakuyepi*), and the
   phrase does not use it. Lakota's own "you and I" is the verb prefix
   *uŋ(k)-*, which is dual, used only for subjects, and opaque; WALS codes
   Lakhota as having no inclusive/exclusive distinction. *Pointer:*
   [../lakota/mitakuye-oyasin-form-and-sound.md](../lakota/mitakuye-oyasin-form-and-sound.md)
   §§1, 5, 6.

**What else these touch.** Points 4 and 5 also bear on the frontmatter's
"with Quechua, Cantonese, Lakota witnesses" (line 2), on §III point 3 (the
claim about 我哋 there holds for "I" only) and on the sentence "The
genealogy stands on Tok Pisin, Quechua, Cantonese, and Lakota" (line 65).
Of the three witnesses this file names, only Quechua stands as a clusivity
witness in the form cited, and its inclusive has no "you" inside it. Among
the forms surveyed, a fully visible you-plus-I belongs to Tok Pisin and its
English-lexified relatives; Pirahã, which has no "we", reaches it only by
joining "I" and "you" in a phrase ([clusivity-survey.md](clusivity-survey.md)
§10).
