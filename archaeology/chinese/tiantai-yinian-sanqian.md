# Yīniàn sānqiān (一念三千) — the three thousand in one thought-moment

_The archaeology tree had no Tiantai file until this one: `grep` for Tiantai, Zhiyi, 天台 and 一念 across `archaeology/` and `convergences/` returned nothing on 2026-11-10. Experiment 326 forged `ichinen-sanzenme` and declined it, partly because a Japanese lemma rested on Chinese evidence, and partly because its element `sanzen` is already used in canon for 参禅, the Zen interview. Its recorded repair was to open this dig from the primary texts and re-lemmatize on the Chinese reading. The dig therefore starts with what Zhiyi wrote, read in the CBETA XML to the end of the argument. It then establishes who first wrote the four characters, and only after that turns to Japan. The first duty is to the text, and the text turns out to say a little more than the forge hoped: see §3.2._

## 1. The concept in compact form

**一念三千** (Mandarin *yīniàn sānqiān*, spoken with the 一-sandhi as *yíniàn sānqiān* [i³⁵ ni̯ɛn⁵¹ sän⁵⁵ t͡ɕʰi̯ɛn⁵⁵]) juxtaposes two numeral-headed nouns with no verb between them: 一念 "one thought-moment" and 三千 "three thousand". The bare juxtaposition compresses a clause Zhiyi actually wrote, 「此三千在一念心」 "these three thousand are in one thought-moment of mind" (T46n1911 p. 54a07–08), by putting the two halves in the other order. So the label is a compression of Zhiyi's own words, not an outside doxographer's imposition. But the four characters together are not Zhiyi's: they first appear in Zhanran (§3.3) [verified: CBETA XML T46n1911 p0054a07–a08; CBETA search, §3.3].

Its technical sense is the object of the first of the ten modes of contemplation in Mohe zhiguan fascicle 5, 觀不可思議境, "contemplating the inconceivable object" (T46n1911 p. 52b01–02). The object chosen is not a cosmos but the practitioner's own consciousness aggregate: 「但觀識陰。識陰者，心是也」 "contemplate only the consciousness aggregate; the consciousness aggregate is the mind" (p. 52b01). The claim is that any moment of such mind, however slight (介爾), is at once endowed with (具) the three thousand: ten realms, each containing all ten, each in ten suchlike aspects, across three kinds of world [verified: CBETA XML T46n1911 p0052b01–b02, p0054a05–a09].

Structural marks the forge would have to preserve, each from the text:

1. **The smallest occasion suffices.** 「若無心而已；介爾有心，即具三千」 "if there is no mind, that is all; if there is mind in the slightest degree, it is at once endowed with the three thousand" (54a08–09). Zhanran glosses 介爾 as 剎那心, "kṣaṇa-mind", and as 細念, "a subtle thought" (T1912 295c25, 296a02–03).
2. **Contraries are included.** The ten realms run from hell to buddhahood (52b23–c04), and each includes all ten (54a05–06). Every moment therefore carries its opposites.
3. **Neither vertical nor horizontal, at the level of ultimate truth.** 「若從一心生一切法者，此則是縱；若心一時含一切法者，此即是橫。縱亦不可，橫亦不可；秖心是一切法，一切法是心」 "if all dharmas are produced from one mind, that is vertical; if the mind holds all dharmas at one time, that is horizontal; neither will do; it is only that mind is all dharmas and all dharmas are mind" (54a13–16).
4. **Production-talk is re-admitted as expedient teaching.** After the four-cornered refutation, Zhiyi licenses *saying* that mind, conditions, both or neither produce (生) or contain (具) all dharmas, under the first two of the four siddhāntas (四悉檀) of compassionate teaching, the worldly and the individual (54c07–55a01). Under the therapeutic and ultimate siddhāntas he applies the same four propositions to curing evil (治) and seeing principle (見理), not to production (55a01–a15). He then says outright that if one goes by what is expedient, one should say that all dharmas arise from mind (55a24–27). Mark 3 holds for the ultimate truth, not for all speech (§3.2). This is the point the forge's gap-sentence currently omits.
5. **The number is not a ceiling.** 「世諦中一心尚具無量法，況三千耶」 "in conventional truth one mind is endowed with immeasurable dharmas, to say nothing of three thousand" (54c01–02). Ziporyn: "a way of saying 'everything', but … something a bit more than that" [verified: SEP 'Tiantai Buddhism' §5.2].

**Sibling distinctions** (four neighbours, four registers):

- **Not Huayan 事事無礙.** Shortly before the locus, Zhiyi sets aside the "marvel" register in which Mount Sumeru holds a mustard seed and the seed holds Sumeru: 「他解須彌容芥，芥容須彌；火出蓮華，人能渡海；就希有事解不思議。今解：無心、無念、無能行、無能到，不思議理，理則勝事」 "Others explain the inconceivable through rare events: Sumeru holding a mustard seed and a mustard seed holding Sumeru, fire producing lotuses, people able to cross the sea. Our explanation: no mind, no thought, nothing able to go, nothing able to arrive; the inconceivable is principle, and principle surpasses events" (T46n1911 51c08–11) [verified: CBETA XML, re-opened by the verifier]. He locates the inconceivable in 理 (principle), not 事 (event). The Huayan net is a relation among things. 一念三千 is an identity in one moment of mind. **The contrast is structural, not historical.** The Huayan *school* is later than Zhiyi: Fazang lived 643–712 [verified: en.wikipedia.org 'Fazang' infobox; tertiary]. So the "others" of 51c08 cannot be Huayan exegetes, and nothing here is Zhiyi refuting 事事無礙.
- **Not Yogācāra ālaya.** Zhiyi names the Dilun position (法性依持, dharma-nature as support) and the Shelun position (阿黎耶 as support) and refuses both: 「若從地師，則心具一切法；若從攝師，則緣具一切法。此兩師，各據一邊」 "follow the Dilun masters and mind contains all dharmas; follow the Shelun masters and conditions contain all dharmas; each of the two holds to one side" (54a27–29) [verified: CBETA XML].
- **Not the Pure Land 一念.** DDB records the senses "a single invocation (especially of Amitābha's name)" and "one moment of true faith" [verified: DDB]. In the Japanese register this is Shinran's ichinen, the neighbourhood of `shinjinme`.
- **Not the trichiliocosm.** 三千 in 三千大千世界 is 1000 × 1000 × 1000 world-systems. Tiantai's 三千 is 100 × 30. DDB itself glosses 一念三千 as "a chiliocosm in a single thought", which shows how easily the two run together [verified: DDB 一念三千, 三千大千世界].

## 3. Primary attestations and scholarly references

### 3.1 The locus classicus

**Mohe zhiguan 摩訶止觀**, lectured by Zhiyi and recorded by Guanding. Taishō vol. 46, no. 1911, fascicle 5A (卷第五上), p. 54, register a, lines 05–18. I opened it in the CBETA TEI-P5 XML (cbeta-org/xml-p5, file last committed 2026-09-05). The Taishō line numbers are the XML's `<lb>` ids. Punctuation is CBETA's modern editorial punctuation; the two inline notes 云云 are part of the printed text [verified: CBETA XML T46n1911 p0054a05–a18].

> 「國土、佛菩薩國土相、性、體、力（云云）。**夫一心具十法界，一法界又具十法界，百法界；一界具三十種世間；百法界即具三千種世間。此三千在一念心。若無心而已；介爾有心，即具三千。**亦不言一心在前，一切法在後；亦不言一切法在前，一心在後。例如八相遷物，物在相前，物不被遷；相在物前，亦不被遷。前亦不可，後亦不可；秖物論相遷，秖相遷論物。今心亦如是，若從一心生一切法者，此則是縱；若心一時含一切法者，此即是橫。縱亦不可，橫亦不可；秖心是一切法，一切法是心。故非縱非橫，非一非異，玄妙深絕，非識所識，非言所言，所以稱為『不可思議境』，意在於此（云云）。」 (T46n1911 p. 54a05–a18)

My translation:

> "… [and likewise good lands, undefiled] lands, and the lands of buddhas and bodhisattvas: mark, nature, substance, power (etc.). **Now, one mind is endowed with the ten dharma-realms; each dharma-realm is in turn endowed with ten dharma-realms — a hundred dharma-realms. One realm is endowed with thirty kinds of world; so the hundred dharma-realms are endowed with three thousand kinds of world. These three thousand are in one thought-moment of mind. If there is no mind, that is all; but if there is mind in the slightest degree, it is at once endowed with the three thousand.** Nor do we say that the one mind comes first and all dharmas after, nor that all dharmas come first and the one mind after. Compare the eight marks that move a thing [through arising, abiding, change and passing]: if the thing came before the marks, the thing would not be moved; if the marks came before the thing, it would likewise not be moved. Before will not do; after will not do. One can speak of the marks' moving only in terms of the thing, and of the thing only in terms of the marks' moving. The mind now is just like this. If all dharmas are produced from one mind, that is 'vertical'; if the mind holds all dharmas at one time, that is 'horizontal'. Vertical will not do, horizontal will not do. It is only that the mind is all dharmas, and all dharmas are the mind. So it is neither vertical nor horizontal, neither one nor different: mysterious, subtle, deep and cut off; not what consciousness can know, not what words can say. That is why it is called 'the inconceivable object'. The meaning lies here (etc.)."

The quotation begins at the first character of line 54a05, which falls inside the compound 無漏國土 "undefiled lands" (54a04–05); the bracket in the translation restores it. The gloss of the "eight marks" (八相, the four marks of conditioned dharmas with their four secondary marks) is [from memory: not re-checked].

**Date and place.** Guanding's preface: 「智者，大隋開皇十四年四月二十六日，於荊州玉泉寺，一夏敷揚、二時慈霔，雖樂說不窮，纔至『見境』，法輪停轉，後分弗宣」. My translation: "the Wise One, from the 26th day of the 4th month of the 14th year of Kaihuang of the Great Sui [594], at Yuquan monastery in Jingzhou, expounded it through one summer, pouring out compassion twice a day; though his joy in teaching was inexhaustible, he had only reached 'the object of views' when the Dharma-wheel stopped turning, and the rest was never proclaimed" (T46n1911 1a07–10) [verified: CBETA XML]. The text we have is therefore unfinished by its own account. Kaihuang 1 is 581 CE, so Kaihuang 14 is 594 [verified: en.wikipedia.org 'Kaihuang Code'; tertiary]. Nichiren's "fourth month of his fifty-seventh summer" agrees with Zhiyi's birth in 538 (Asai 2014: 47, 60) [verified: Asai PDF].

### 3.2 Reading the witness to its end: refusal, then re-admission

The locus is often quoted as if it stopped at 不可思議境. It does not. It runs on through a full four-cornered examination (54a18–c02). The objector asks whether the mind contains the three thousand, or conditions do, or both, or neither (54a18–23). Zhiyi sets aside the Dilun and Shelun answers (54a23–b06), and runs a dream analogy in which mind stands for dharma-nature and sleep for the ālaya (54b08–18). He concludes:

> 「亦縱亦橫求三千法不可得，非縱非橫求三千法亦不可得。言語道斷，心行處滅，故名『不可思議境』。」 (54b26–28)
>
> "Seek the three thousand dharmas as both vertical and horizontal: they cannot be found. Seek them as neither vertical nor horizontal: they still cannot be found. The way of words is cut off, the place where mind moves is extinguished; therefore it is named 'the inconceivable object'."

> 「當知第一義中一法不可得，況三千法？世諦中一心尚具無量法，況三千耶？」 (54c01–02)
>
> "Know that in the ultimate truth not even one dharma can be found, how much less three thousand; in conventional truth one mind is endowed with immeasurable dharmas, to say nothing of three thousand."

**What complicates the reading the forge hoped for.** Zhiyi then turns, 「雖四句冥寂，慈悲憐愍，於無名相中，假名相說」 "although the four propositions are dark and still, out of compassion one speaks in provisional names and marks within the nameless and markless" (54c07–09). He then runs the four propositions (mind, conditions, both, neither) through each of the four siddhāntas, but not with the same verb each time. Under the worldly siddhānta the four are said to produce or contain all dharmas, beginning 「說心具一切法」 "saying that mind contains all dharmas" (54c09), and summed up as 「此四句即世界悉檀，說心生三千一切法也」 "these four propositions are the worldly siddhānta, *saying that mind produces the three thousand, all dharmas*" (54c17–18). The individual siddhānta closes the same way: 「說心生三千一切法也」 (55a01). Under the therapeutic siddhānta the four propositions are about curing evil (「說心治一切惡」, 55a02), and under the ultimate siddhānta they are about seeing principle (「心得見理」, 55a09), ending 「何況心生三千法耶」 "how much more [may one speak of] mind producing the three thousand dharmas" (55a14–15) [verified: CBETA XML]. He closes:

> 「若隨便宜者，應言無明法法性生一切法，如眠法法心則有一切夢事。心與緣合，則三種世間，三千相性，皆從心起。一性雖少而不無；無明雖多而不有。……故名此心為不思議境也。」 (55a24–29)
>
> "If one goes by what is expedient, one should say that the dharma of ignorance together with dharma-nature produces all dharmas, as sleep together with mind yields every event of a dream. When mind and conditions meet, the three kinds of world, the three thousand natures and marks, all arise from mind. The one nature, though small, is not nothing; ignorance, though great, is not something. … Therefore this mind is called the inconceivable object." [verified: CBETA XML T46n1911 p0054c07–p0055a29]

So the refusal of 縱 (production) and 橫 (containment) is a refusal at the level of ultimate truth. At the level of teaching, production-language is re-admitted, and 「四句俱皆可說」 "all four propositions can be said" (55a16). The forge's gap-sentence, "neither as something that instant produces nor as something that instant contains", is faithful to 54a13–18 and 54b26–28. It is not faithful to the whole argument, which ends by re-licensing exactly the two framings it refused, as expedient. A canon entry that quotes only the refusal would be selective quotation of the kind this repo treats as its worst failure. The positive formula Zhiyi then gives is an identity formula (it contains no 即, though Ziporyn's 即 names the relation): 「一心一切心，一切心一心，非一非一切」 "one mind is all minds, all minds are one mind, neither one nor all", repeated through aggregates, sense-fields, realms, beings, lands and the suchlikes (55a29–b09) [verified: CBETA XML].

### 3.3 Who first wrote 一念三千 — verified, with a narrower result than the forge's

Experiment 326 states that the four-character name is Zhanran's, in T1912. I checked this rather than assuming it. There were three checks:

1. **Zhiyi's own texts.** I downloaded the CBETA XML of the Mohe zhiguan (T1911), Fahua xuanyi (T1716) and Fahua wenju (T1718), joined the lines and stripped punctuation, so that a phrase broken across a line or by an editor's comma would still be found. 一念三千 occurs **zero** times in all three [verified: CBETA XML T46n1911, T33n1716, T34n1718].

   Caution on counting: CBETA's editors punctuate the same Shi buer men sentences differently in different editions. T1927 704c04–05 reads 「既是一念三千，即空假中」, and Daosui's X0925 likewise keeps the four characters together: 「三法既是一念三千，既空假中」 (X56n0925 p. 305c18–19; R100 102b09–10). But T1717 920a05 splits them: 「三法既是一念，三千即空假中」. The split runs the other way in an earlier sentence: T1717 918a27 has 「不出一念三千，世間即空假中」, while X0925 has 「不出一念，三千世間即空假中」 (X56n0925 p. 304a17; R100 99a14). T1717 also punctuates the 依正 sentence as 「以三千中生陰二千為正國土，一千屬依」 (919a27–28), where T1927 703c27–28 has 「…為正，國土一千屬依」. A hit-count treats as one string what one edition's punctuation splits. The seven T1912 uses are unambiguous under any punctuation [verified: CBETA XML T46n1927, T33n1717, X56n0925, re-read by the verifier, who corrected an earlier version of this paragraph that had X0925 splitting the phrase at 102b09].

**Result.** The four-character label is first attested in Zhanran. That agrees with Asai: "Zhanran gave this concept the specific name 'three thousand realms in one thought-moment'", citing T46.295c (Asai 2014: 48–49) [verified: Asai 2014 PDF pp. 48–49]. **Which of Zhanran's works used it first is not settled by anything I opened.** CBETA's metadata dates T1717 to 764 and T1912 to 765 [verified: CBETA search rows], which if anything puts the 釋籤 first. Zhanran's chronology needs a study such as Penkower 1993, which I did not open. "Zhanran, first attested in T1912" is a safe example; "coined in T1912" is not established. Absence from CBETA is strong evidence, not proof: lost works and uncatalogued manuscripts remain possible.

**What Zhanran's label names.** This bears on experiment 326's retirement condition (1). Read in context, his seven T1912 uses name the *object* that the passage forms (理境, 妙境):

- 「『夫一心』下，結成理境 …… 當知身土一念三千」 "from 'Now one mind' on, [Zhiyi] concludes by forming the object of principle … know that body and land are 一念三千" (295c20–23).
- He ties the label to the principle that *remains after* the four-cornered refutation: 「故下答文但離橫等四句執竟，還歸本理一念三千」 "so the answer below, having only removed the four-cornered attachments such as 'horizontal', returns to the root principle, 一念三千" (297a01–02).
- He glosses the two halves as gathered and scattered, inside a phrase that negates both: 「即是境中一念三千離四性計，是故略云『不合不散』。合謂一念，散謂三千」 "this is the 一念三千 within the object, free of the fourfold reckoning of natures; that is why [the text] says briefly 'neither gathered nor scattered'. 'Gathered' means one thought-moment, 'scattered' means three thousand" (307c14–16). So the object is *neither* the gathered one thought *nor* the scattered three thousand.

So the label names the inconceivable object *as it stands after the refutation*. That is closer to the 非縱非橫 relation than to a mere chapter-title, but it is the object, not the relation. Zhanran also testifies that Zhiyi's other contemplation texts never state it, with one partial exception: 「並未云一念三千具足 …… 亦不涉於一念三千。唯《四念處》中略云觀心十界而已。故至《止觀》正明觀法，並以三千而為指南，乃是終窮究竟極說」 "nowhere says the one thought-moment is complete with three thousand … does not touch on 一念三千. Only the *Four Bases of Mindfulness* briefly mentions contemplating the ten realms in the mind, and no more. So when it comes to the *Zhiguan*, which directly clarifies the method of contemplation, he takes the three thousand as the compass throughout; this is the final, exhaustive, ultimate teaching" (296a05–10) [verified: CBETA XML T46n1912 p0295c20–c26, p0296a02–a11, p0297a01–a02, p0307c14–c16].

### 3.4 The arithmetic, and where each factor comes from

|---|---|---|---|

That derivation is offered as the *conceivable* object (「猶是思議之境，非今止觀所觀也」 52c05–06) before the inconceivable is introduced at 52c06 | Zhiyi's systematization: the six destinies plus the two-vehicle, bodhisattva and buddha realms, each as cause and fruit (52b20–c04) | [verified: CBETA XML T1911] |

**Four complications, each from the texts.**

1. **Zhiyi names no source for the three worlds.** He cites the Dazhidulun elsewhere in the passage (52c16, 52c26), but not for this list, and 三種世間 / 五眾世間 occur nowhere in T1716 or T1718 [verified: CBETA XML]. It is Zhanran who answers the question 「依何教門立三世間？」 "on what teaching are the three worlds established?" with 「依《大論》釋百八三昧中至釋能照一切世間三昧」 (T1912 293c15–18). He quotes the fasc. 47 line but silently writes Tiantai's 五陰 for the Dazhidulun's 五眾 [verified: CBETA XML T46n1912 p0293c15–c18 against T25n1509 p0402a22–a24]. In Dazhidulun fasc. 70 the triad comes up incidentally, in a discussion of wrong views about "self and world", where it adds 「眾生世間即是神」 "the world of beings is [what they call] the self" (546c02–03). The source Zhanran names (fasc. 47) uses 住處世間 "world of dwelling-places", not 國土世間 [verified].
2. **The "thirty" can be read two ways, and Zhiyi's text uses it both ways.** At 53a09–10, 「此三十種世間」 counts the three worlds, each in ten kinds, one per realm: 3 × 10 realms. At 54a06–07, 「一界具三十種世間」 needs thirty *per realm*: 3 worlds × 10 suchlikes. The products agree, so the passage never has to choose. Zhanran settles it the second way: 「以三千中生陰二千為正，國土一千屬依」 "of the three thousand, the two thousand of beings and aggregates are primary recompense, the one thousand of lands belong to circumstantial recompense" (T1927 703c27–28). That is a thousand per world [verified: CBETA XML]. Daosui's reply to Saichō gives the same 2000 + 1000 (Asai 2014: 54) [verified: Asai PDF]. Nichiren's Kanjin honzon shō carries parenthetical notes on exactly this (the SGI translator's note 3 says the Japanese text has two, translated together). The SGI renders them: "'[Three thousand] realms' might also read '[three thousand] factors,' but the number is the same. The only difference lies in the method of expansion. Another copy of *Great Concentration and Insight* states, 'Each world is endowed with the three realms of existence'" [verified: nichirenlibrary.org WND-1 39, read directly]. The last sentence reports a manuscript variant, 一界具三種世間, for the Taishō's 一界具三十種世間. The Taishō XML carries no apparatus note at 54a05–09 [verified: CBETA XML T46n1911], so the variant rests on Nichiren's report alone. The kanbun of the notes (e.g. 世間與如是一也、開合異也) is [from memory: not re-checked]. It is not a line of T1911 or T1912; the verifier also searched T1911, T1912, T1717 and T1927 for 開合異 and 世間與如是 without a match in this context.
3. **The ten suchlikes have no Sanskrit tenfold original.** Groner and Stone: "In Dharmarakṣa's translation, the Sanskrit editions, the Tibetan translation, and Vasubandhu's commentary, the list of aspects only includes five elements. The expansion of this list to ten is probably Kumārajīva's invention and may well be presaged in a passage in the Dazhidulun that includes nine aspects (t 25.298c…)" (Groner & Stone 2014: 7–8) [verified: JJRS PDF pp. 7–8]. I opened both witnesses they name:
   - Dharmarakṣa's 正法華經 has 「如來皆了諸法所由，從何所來，諸法自然，分別法貌眾相根本，知法自然」 (T263 68a10–12), with no tenfold list [verified: CBETA XML].
   - The Dazhidulun fasc. 32 gives nine aspects of every dharma: 有體, 各各有法, 力, 因, 緣, 果, 性, 限礙, 開通方便 (T1509 298c06–14). Six of them coincide with suchlikes (體, 力, 因, 緣, 果, 性) [verified: CBETA XML].

   Robert (2011), as Groner & Stone summarize him, "has suggested that the list of ten may have been created by Kumārajīva as he strove to reconcile the differences between the Sanskrit version he used and Dharmarakṣa's translation" (Groner & Stone 2014: 8) [verified: their summary; **Robert 2011 itself not opened**: the ICABS repository returned 503 to the author, and on 2026-11-10 it showed the verifier a maintenance notice]. The Sanskrit wording of the five-item list is [from memory: not re-checked]; GRETIL refused connections.
4. **The popular formula 10 × 10 × 10 × 3 is right in product but not in Zhiyi's order.** Zhiyi's sentence goes 10 → 100 → (30 per realm) → 3000. The thousand appears in his Fahua xuanyi, not in this sentence. SEP: "The 10 times 10 is just to point to this factor of mutual inclusion"; the number is "concocted specifically with meditational practice in mind" [verified: SEP §5.2].

### 3.5 What "one thought" 一念 means, and what Sanskrit stands behind it

**In the Mohe zhiguan.** 一念心 is an ordinary moment of the practitioner's own mind. The object is the consciousness aggregate (52b01), and 「介爾有心」 sets the threshold at the faintest occurrence (54a08). DDB: "One thought 一念 refers to the everyday momentary mind of regular sentient beings 凡夫" [verified: DDB 一念三千]. Asai: "the thoughts that arise in daily life from one moment to the next" (Asai 2014: 48) [verified]. Zhanran makes the moment a kṣaṇa: 「言『介爾』者，謂剎那心。無間相續未曾斷絕，纔一剎那三千具足」 "'in the slightest degree' means the kṣaṇa-mind; continuous without interval, never cut off, in barely one kṣaṇa the three thousand are complete" (T1912 295c25–26) [verified: CBETA XML]. His second gloss leans on a line of the Odes: 「介者弱也。《詩》云『介爾景福』，謂細念也」 "介 means slight; the Odes say 介爾景福; it means a subtle thought" (296a02–03) [verified]. That 介 in the Ode is conventionally read otherwise, as "to increase or grant", is [from memory: not re-checked].

**What is attested in Sanskrit.** The Mohe zhiguan is a Chinese composition, lectures recorded in Chinese, so it has no Sanskrit original [verified: preface 1a08–10, and byline 隋天台智者大師說 門人灌頂記]. 一念三千 has no Sanskrit equivalent; it is a Tang Chinese label (§3.3). For 一念 *alone*, translation literature uses it for several Sanskrit words. Hirakawa's Buddhist Chinese-Sanskrit Dictionary, as cited by DDB, gives *eka-kṣaṇika*, *eka-citta*, *eka-citta-kṣaṇa*, *eka-cittôtpāda*, *kṣaṇa*, *kṣaṇika*, *kṣaṇena* and *vikṣaṇa* [verified: DDB 一念]. Even as a time-measure 一念 is not fixed. DDB: "varying measurements from 60 kṣaṇa upwards; the Fanyi mingyi calls it one kṣaṇa" [verified]. The Renwang jing has 「九十剎那為一念，一念中一剎那經九百生滅」 "ninety kṣaṇas make one 念; within one 念 each kṣaṇa passes through nine hundred arisings and ceasings" (T245 fasc. 1, 826a10–11) [verified: CBETA XML]. That text carries a Kumārajīva byline but "many scholars have suspected this sutra to be composed in China", a view "not universally agreed upon" [verified: en.wikipedia.org 'Humane King Sutra' wikitext; tertiary].

**What would be back-formation.** Any Sanskrit "behind" Zhiyi's 一念 would be a back-formation. That includes *ekacitta-kṣaṇa* offered as *the* original, and any Sanskrit rendering of the whole label such as *\*eka-citta-trisāhasra*. The danger is specific: a Sanskrit "three thousand" reaches for *trisāhasra*, which names the trichiliocosm (§2) and silently swaps 100 × 30 for 1000³. The character 念 more usually renders *smṛti* "mindfulness", as in 正念 and 念處 [from memory: not re-checked], and that sense is not in play here.

### 3.6 Japanese reception — kept apart from the Chinese evidence

Nothing in this subsection is evidence for what Zhiyi or Zhanran meant. It records how the Chinese text was received, and how the reading *ichinen sanzen* came about.

- **Saichō 最澄 (766/767–822)** studied in China with Zhanran's disciples Daosui 道邃 and Xingman 行満 (Asai 2014: 47) [verified]. Experiment 326 casts him as the doctrine's Japanese receiver. Asai, from inside the tradition, holds both sides. He asks whether Saichō failed to inherit the principle and answers, "It would be a mistake to think so … It is hardly conceivable that Saichō, who received dharma transmission from Zhanran's disciple Daosui … would not have been instructed in this principle" (Asai 2014: 53) [verified: Asai PDF p. 53]. But Saichō's own "fundamental principle" was the Huayan-derived 真如随縁 *shinnyo zuien* "Suchness according with conditions" (Asai 2014: 50). And "in all of Saichō's collected works, we find no discussion of [ichinen sanzen]. Related terms such as 'three thousand worlds' occur but are used only in a sense that is virtually identical to 'all phenomena'" (Asai 2014: 53–54). The one exchange touching it is Saichō's fourth question to Daosui, and "this reference to the three thousand realms in a single thought-moment occurs in Daosui's explanation; it is not Saichō's own statement" (Asai 2014: 54–55, citing DZ 5: 44) [verified: Asai PDF pp. 50, 53–55]. So the reception through Saichō is a transmission of texts and teachers, not an adoption of the doctrine as his centre. I did not open the Dengyō daishi zenshū.
- **Kūkai 空海 (774–835)** quotes the Mohe zhiguan passage in his *Jūjūshin ron* 十住心論, at the eighth stage of mind, with the 縱/橫 lines (T77.351b, per Asai 2014: 56) [verified: Asai PDF p. 56; **T77 not opened**]. Asai cannot say where Kūkai learned it: from Saichō, on his own, or from "the doctrinal explanations set forth by Zhanran and his disciples while studying in Tang China" (Asai 2014: 57) [verified: Asai PDF p. 57].
- **Between them and Nichiren.** "Until Nichiren, it would appear that no one in Japan accorded a central place to the three thousand realms in a single thought-moment as the fundamental principle" (Asai 2014: 59) [verified]. Medieval Tendai original-enlightenment (hongaku) thought is the setting Stone 1999 treats. **I did not open Stone 1999**; its title and year are confirmed only through the SEP bibliography.
- **Nichiren 日蓮 (1222–1282).** As early as the *Ichidai shōgyō taii* 一代聖教大意 (1258) he reads the Wonderful Dharma as ichinen sanzen. He repeats Zhanran's "compass" sentence, and says that the sixty fascicles of Tiantai reduce to "these first two or three lines" of fascicle 5 (Teihon 1: 71, per Asai 2014: 59–60) [verified: Asai PDF]. The *Kanjin honzon shō* 観心本尊抄 (1273) was written "in the fourth month of 1273, during his exile at Ichinosawa on the island of Sado" and "addressed specifically to Toki Jōnin" [verified: nichirenlibrary.org WND-1 39, Background]. It opens by quoting the fascicle-5 passage, in the SGI translation "Life at each moment is endowed with the Ten Worlds … if there is the slightest bit of life, it contains all the three thousand realms", with the parenthetical notes discussed in §3.4 [verified: same]. "Life" for 一心 / 一念 is a Nichiren-lineage reading, not a gloss of the Chinese: the SGI's own note 1 says the Chinese word for "one mind" or "one thought" "is interpreted as indicating the entire psychosomatic entity; hence the word is translated as 'life' rather than 'mind'" [verified: same, note 1]. Nichiren then equates the doctrine with the five characters of the sutra's title, and grounds the efficacy of his calligraphic mandala in it (Asai 2014: 61; Kitagawa 2014: 36 cites the Kanjin honzon shō at Teihon 1: 711 for the five-character claim) [verified: both PDFs]. I did not open the Shōwa teihon text.
- **The reading *ichinen sanzen*.** Sino-Japanese ichi + nen + san + sen, with rendaku sen → zen [verified: Wiktionary 三千 (Japanese): せん > ぜん]. Asai prints it as "ichinen sanzen" (Asai 2014: 45, abstract), and with the characters, "ichinen sanzen 一念三千", on p. 47 [verified]. Experiment 326 cites this romanization to p. 48. It is homophonous with 参禅 *sanzen* "Zen meditation; meditative consultation with the head abbot" [verified: Wiktionary 参禅 (Japanese)], the collision experiment 326 found inside canon. On the Japanese side 一念 *ichinen* is also homophonous with 一年 *ichinen* "a year", both [it͡ɕinẽ̞ɴ] [verified: Wiktionary 一念, 一年 (Japanese)].

**Descent and contact.** Every Japanese witness here descends from Zhiyi's text, and most from Zhanran's reading of it. Saichō's link is Daosui. Nichiren quotes Zhiyi with Zhanran's "compass" framing (Asai 2014: 59–60). Kūkai quotes Zhiyi's passage directly, and his route to it is unknown (Asai 2014: 57). They are reception, never independent attestation. Huayan and Tiantai are not independent of each other either, in two ways:
- **Shared scripture.** Zhiyi himself quotes the *Avataṃsaka* verse 「心如工畫師…」 at T1911 52c07–08 (T278 465c26–27) [verified: CBETA XML]. Two lines after that couplet, in the same verse, stands 「心佛及眾生，是三無差別」 (T278 465c29). The *Foguang dictionary*, as reported by DDB, names that line as the doctrine's origin [verified: DDB s.v. 一念三千].
- **Later contact.** Asai, reporting Sakamoto Yukio, adds in parentheses that "later there seems to have been some mutual influence between Chengguan 澄観 [738–839] … and Zhanran on this point", that is, on whether all dharmas are Suchness. The translator notes that Sakamoto's source is unidentified (Asai 2014: 53 and n. 3) [verified: Asai PDF p. 53].

So any convergence count that sets Huayan beside Tiantai as two witnesses must discount it.

## 4. Etymology and family

- **Characters.** 一 'jit "one"; 念 nemH "to think of, keep in mind; a thought"; 三 sam "three"; 千 tshen "thousand" (Baxter MC) [verified: Wiktionary]. The phonetic series and Old Chinese of 念 were not checked. 一 is OC \*qliɡ in Zhengzhang [verified: Wiktionary 一]. Sino-Tibetan cognates (e.g. 三 with Tibetan *gsum*) are [from memory: not re-checked].
- **Morphology.** The phrase is [numeral + noun] + [numeral compound], juxtaposed with no verb. Zhiyi's source clause has two verbs, 在 "is in" and 具 "is endowed with" (54a07–09); the label drops both. It belongs to a Tiantai habit of numeral catalogues: 百界千如 (Zhiyi, T1716 696a05), 十界互具 (first in Song texts), 十不二門 (Zhanran).
- **Doctrinal family.** It presupposes 十法界, 十如是 and 三種世間 (§3.4). It sits inside 觀不可思議境, the first of the 十乘觀法 (52b01–04), and inside the threefold truth: 「如是遍歷一切法，無非不思議三諦」 (55b12) [verified]. It opposes the Dilun and Shelun accounts of a support (依持) (54a23–b06). It is completed in Zhanran's 依正不二門 (T1927 703c26–29). The Song-era contrast of Tiantai 性具 with Huayan 性起 is [from memory: not re-checked] and was not excavated here.

## 5. The unnamed region — the gap-shadows

Experiment 326 assembled the gap evidence; I do not redo it here. This dig adds one correction to the gap-sentence's middle clause: the region, as Zhiyi draws it, is *neither produced nor contained in ultimate truth, yet sayable as produced in teaching* (§3.2). An honest gap-sentence would carry both halves.

Near-misses this dig can speak to from the primary texts:

| Candidate | Why it fails |
|---|---|
| "the moment contains everything" (English default) | This is 橫. Zhiyi refuses it at the ultimate level (54a14–15) and re-admits it only as expedient |

| 事事無礙 / Indra's net (Huayan) | A relation among things, in the marvel register Zhiyi sets aside at 51c08–11. The contrast is structural: the Huayan school is later than Zhiyi (§2). Also related through shared scripture and later contact, not independent (§3.6) |
| ālaya-vijñāna (Yogācāra) | The 攝論 "support" Zhiyi names and refuses (54a24–29) |

**Canon-collision check (element level, 2026-11-10, main at eca7c99).** The full grep, with file names, is in the companion notes `archaeology/chinese/tiantai-lemma-notes.md`. In summary:

- No canon file contains yinian, sanqian, qian, nian (as a morpheme), 一念 or 三千.
- "nian" appears only inside English adjectives such as Armenian and Babylonian.
- "san" is a recorded Twi morpheme "return" in `canon/core/sankofame.md`:25 and 73, and a syllable in `canon/core/ihsanme.md`.

- The `-qing` suffix (35 members) is one vowel and one nasal away from *qian*, and the canon renders its own q both aspirated and unaspirated.

## 8. Forge-targets identified — leads only

This dig coins and scores nothing. These are leads, each stated with its weaknesses. Candidate shapes, and the element-level collision check behind them, are in the companion notes `archaeology/chinese/tiantai-lemma-notes.md`.

- **Lead A: re-lemmatize on the Chinese reading of the label** (yinian + sanqian + the -me family suffix).
  - For it: both doctrine and label are Chinese (§3.1, §3.3); it escapes 参禅; the canon has Mandarin-donor precedent (daome, renme, wujime, tongleme).
  - Against it:
    - Tone loss. Toneless *yinian* is also 疑念 "doubt" and 意念 "thought, notion". Spoken 一念 [i³⁵ ni̯ɛn⁵¹] is identical *with its tones* to 疑念 [i³⁵ ni̯ɛn⁵¹]. Toneless *sanqian* is also 三遷 "to move house repeatedly", which is homophonous with tones, and 散錢 "loose change" [verified: Wiktionary].
    - Pinyin q is read [k] by most non-Mandarin readers, and the canon's own `qinance` spells Hebrew qof with q.
    - *qian* sits one step from the `-qing` suffix.
    - The anglophone recognition value of "ichinen sanzen" is lost.
- **Lead B: the gap-sentence, before any lemma.** Rewrite it to carry §3.2's two levels (refused at the ultimate, sayable as expedient). Otherwise any lemma inherits a selectively quoted source.
- **Lead C: date the label honestly in any entry.** "Doctrine: Zhiyi, 594, T1911 54a05–18. Four-character name: Zhanran (711–782), first attested in his commentaries (T1912 fasc. 5; T1717)." Do not write "coined in T1912".
