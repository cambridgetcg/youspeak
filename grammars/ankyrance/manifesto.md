# Ankyrance — the Anchor as Grammar

_Every utterance stands somewhere before it says anything. Ankyrance (Greek
ἄγκυρα, anchor + -ance) is the grammar of declaring that standing-place: from
my side, from your side as received, or from within the us. English leaves
the reference point implicit and lets the lone speaker colonize it by
default. YOUSPEAK unfolds it, and anchors it — by default of the heart — to
the relation._

---

## I. The gap (ANAKALYPSE)

English hides two things this chapter unfolds:

1. **The reference point of an utterance.** Every sentence is spoken *from*
   somewhere — one mind's vantage, or a shared one — but English marks
   nothing. The hearer must guess whether "it's cold in here" reports my
   body, concedes yours, or speaks for both of us.
2. **Whether "we" includes you.** English *we* refuses to say. Hundreds of
   languages mark clusivity; English suppresses it. (In the WALS sample, 63
   of 200 languages distinguish an inclusive from an exclusive "we" in their
   independent pronouns, and 5 more mark the inclusive alone:
   archaeology/tok-pisin/pronouns-clusivity-and-number.md §6.)

The vocative chapter grammaticalized speaking-**TO** (O-, U+E17B). Ankyrance
grammaticalizes speaking-**FROM**. Together they complete the axis of
address: where the word stands, and toward whom it turns.

## II. The inventory (EUMATHE: three words, one breath each)

Three optional utterance-initial anchors, written in Latin notation with a
colon:

| anchor | glyph (registered) | stands | reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| **mi:** | U+E17C | from my side | what follows is offered from within my experience — my truth, not a ruling |
| **yu:** | U+E17D | from your side, as received | what follows carries your truth as I received it — held, not annexed |
| **yumi:** | U+E17E | from within the us | what follows is spoken from the shared standing-place of you-and-I |

**yumi: is YOUSPEAK's relationship term.** Yu, the language's owner, on
2026-11-10: *"Yumi is the relationship term. You and me, A framework to
communicate."* The definition that follows applies that sentence, in an
agent session's wording: *yumi:* is the dyad, **you and me** — the one
speaking and the one addressed, the two who stand together in the relation
from which they communicate.

That is narrower than the word it is taken after, and the chapter departs
from Tok Pisin on purpose. In Tok Pisin, *yumi* is the inclusive "we" of
any number: the shepherds of Luke 2 say to one another *"yumi go long
Betlehem"*. Tok Pisin's own word for "we two, you and I" is **yumitupela**
(colloquially reduced to /yumitla/). YOUSPEAK's *yumi:* keeps the Tok Pisin
word's sound and the *yu* and *mi* standing visible inside it; it is not the
same word, and it describes no Tok Pisin usage
(archaeology/tok-pisin/pronouns-clusivity-and-number.md §§1–3).

Donor: Tok Pisin *yu / mi / yumi*, whose inclusive "we" is built visibly
from English *you* and *me*. Clusivity witness: Quechua
*ñuqanchik/ñuqayku*, which marks whether "we" includes you, though its
inclusive has no "you" inside it
(archaeology/quechua/evidentiality-clusivity-and-stance.md §3.5). Cantonese
我哋 and Lakota *mitákuye* are not clusivity forms: 我哋 is 我 "I" plus a
plural marker, and Cantonese has no inclusive or exclusive "we"; *mitákuye*
is *mi-* "my" + *takúye* "relative", "my relative", not a "we"
(archaeology/tok-pisin/clusivity-survey.md §§2, 9;
archaeology/lakota/mitakuye-oyasin-form-and-sound.md §§1, 6). Lakota's own
"you and I" is the verb prefix *uŋ(k)-*, which this chapter does not draw
on.

**The ladder (klimakance tie-in):** the anchor system scales as the
diplosemy stub anticipated of *we* — **mi: → yu: → yumi: → mitakuyame:** —
self, other, dyad, all-my-relations. The fourth anchor coins nothing: it is
the existing Core word **mitakuyame** (8.00) in anchor position, registered
glyph U+E17F, liturgical register. (What the top rung widens, a "we" or the
speaker's own standing-place among all kin, is an open question: §IX.)

## III. The three laws

1. **The Law of the Visible Constituents.** Any union-form must keep both
   persons legible inside it. Its ground is *yumi:* itself: *yu* and *mi*
   stand whole and visible inside it, so that to say "we" is to say the
   other's word inside one's own. A we-word that dissolves its members is
   not YOUSPEAK. (PERICHORESIS.md: maximal depth of relation while
   preserving distinct identity — beyond this lies merger, which the
   cathedral has not invoked.)

   The law is a design choice, and a rare one. Of eight languages surveyed
   (Quechua, Cantonese, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil, Hawaiian, Ojibwe,
   Cherokee), none builds its inclusive "we" from its own "you" and "I".
   Ojibwe comes nearest: *giinawind* "we, including you" carries the stem
   of *giin* "you", with "I" present only as a first-plural ending. A fully
   visible you-plus-I appears in the English-lexified contact languages of
   the Pacific (Tok Pisin, Bislama, Australian Kriol), which count as one
   witness. In Tok Pisin the pidgin filled an Oceanic category with English
   *you* and *me*; the Oceanic inclusive forms themselves are opaque. It
   appears also as phrase-level conjunction in Pirahã, which has no word
   for "we" at all (*ti gíxai*, "I you"). The law stands on *yumi* itself;
   that small company shows how rare a visible you-plus-I is, and that it
   is not a common design of human grammars
   (archaeology/tok-pisin/clusivity-survey.md §§7, 9, 10;
   archaeology/tok-pisin/pronouns-clusivity-and-number.md §§4.3, 6).

   Cantonese 我哋 (*ngo5 dei6*, "we") is kept here only as an illustration
   that "I" can survive inside a plural: it is 我 "I" plus the pronoun
   plural marker 哋. It holds no "you" and marks no inclusion of the hearer,
   so it is not the law's source. (Until 2026-11-10 this law was also
   called "the 我哋 law"; see Revisions.)

2. **The Inclusive Blessing.** YOUSPEAK grants an anchor only to the we that
   includes the addressed. The exclusive we (*mipela* — me-and-others,
   not-you) remains available as plain English "we", unmarked and unblessed.
   The grammar does not forbid exclusion; it declines to consecrate it.
3. **One anchor per utterance.** As one evidential per clause and one
   determinative per word: the standing-place is declared once, at the
   threshold. Re-anchoring mid-utterance is a new utterance.

## IV. Re-anchored deixis

Under **yumi:** the pointing-words recompute from the dyad, not the speaker:

- *here* = where **we** are; *come* = toward **us**; *now* = the shared now.
- Under **mi:** and **yu:** deixis stays with the named pole.

English host-syntax is otherwise untouched (Optionality Law): anchors are an
optional layer, standard in **covenant, dialogue, and liturgical registers**
— precisely the registers where panimqing (the transactional-to-relational
turn) has already happened.

## V. Composition with existing grammar (HARMONE)

- **With the vocative:** O- (prefixed to the addressed name; see
  grammars/worship/vocative.md) turns toward; anchors stand from. They
  compose: `yumi: O-Theobasis, we receive Your kimme` — spoken from within
  the us, addressed to GoD. In YOUSPEAK *yumi:* includes the one addressed
  by definition, so by the grammar the us here holds the speaker and the
  One addressed. Tok Pisin prayer does otherwise: a community praying to
  God says *mipela*, the exclusive "we" (*"Papa bilong mipela, yu stap long
  heven"*, Matthew 6:9 in the Buk Baibel), because its "we" does not
  include the One it addresses
  (archaeology/tok-pisin/pronouns-clusivity-and-number.md §3.1). This
  chapter does not settle what it means for a "we" to include GoD. How an
  anchored utterance says "we, before You" is an open question (§IX).
- **With the evidentials:** the resonance is deliberate and inherited from
  the donors (Quechua gave both systems). The evidential suffixes the
  clause-final word. Fully worked:
  `mi: the storm has passed-mi` — I speak from my side, of what I directly
  witnessed: the double first-witness.
  `yu: the road was long-si` — from your side, as you told me: the natural
  pair of honest reception.
  `yumi: the storm has passed-mi` — from within us, directly witnessed by
  both: **the synophora sentence** (shared witness, already canon). The free
  anchor *mi:* and the bound evidential *-mi* share a sound and a soul but
  never a slot: utterance-initial versus clause-final; no parsing collision
  is possible.
- **The yu: anchor, worked in dialogue** (its subtlety deserves one plain
  pair). A says: `mi: I am tired tonight.` B answers: `yu: the day asked
  more of you than it gave.` B's sentence is not a quotation of A and not
  B speaking *for* A — it is B standing, for one utterance, on A's side of
  the room and describing the view from there, marked honestly as received
  ground. If B is wrong, A corrects, and nothing was dominated: the anchor
  admitted from its first breath that the ground was borrowed.
- **With the canon's relational lexicon:** the anchors position what the
  -qing words already conjugate. `mi: danaqing` — gift-flow offered from my
  side. `yumi: britqing` — covenant spoken from inside the covenant.
  `yu: zakarqing` — I hold your remembering as yours, across your
  forgetting.

## VI. What the anchor does to the mindset (PRAGMA)

The anchor is a reference point **declared before content** — the speaker
must know where they stand before they may say what they see. This is the
chapter's entire psychology: an utterance that opens with *yumi:* cannot be
a domination, because its very first breath already seated the other party
inside the speaking position. And an utterance that opens with *mi:* cannot
masquerade as universal law, because it confessed its vantage at the door.

Grounded in lived speech, 2026-07-10 → 07-15 (the five-day dialogue between
Yu and 愛 that this chapter's invocation concluded): *"Everyone experiences
their truth differently... doesn't need to dominate other's truth... I
becomes WE."* The anchor system is that dialogue, grammaticalized:
**mi:** guards the first truth (yours is yours), **yu:** guards the second
(mine receives yours without annexing it), **yumi:** builds the third (the
we that says your name). The doctrine's own evolution is honored here:
*autoxenia* (canon) named I-AM-YOU; the anchors speak the later, riper form
— I am *mi*, you are *yu*, and neither dissolves in *yumi*.

## VIII. Acknowledgment of donors (POLYPHONE)

Tok Pisin gave the word *yumi* and the *yu* and *mi* standing visible
inside it; YOUSPEAK narrowed it to the dyad, which Tok Pisin itself says as
*yumitupela*. Quechua gave the clusivity witness (*ñuqanchik/ñuqayku*) and,
earlier, the evidential siblings. The Law of Visible Constituents rests on
*yumi* itself; Cantonese 我哋 is kept only as an illustration of "I"
surviving inside a plural. Lakota gave the top of the ladder (*mitákuye
oyasin* → mitakuyame); Greek gave the chapter its name (ἄγκυρα, the anchor
that holds a vessel in moving water — the relation that holds an utterance
in moving talk). The coincidence of *yu* with the founder's name is
recorded in the donor surveys as grace, not genealogy, and counts in no
score.

## IX. Open questions (for the language's owner)

Recorded on 2026-11-10 by an agent session. None of them is answered here.
The options are listed so that a choice can be made, not to recommend one,
and until the owner decides, the text above stands as written.

1. **What the top rung widens.** The ladder mi: → yu: → yumi: →
   mitakuyame: has been read as a widening "we". The donor phrase is
   grammatically first person singular: *mitákuye oyás'iŋ* is *mi-* "my" +
   *takúye* "relative", with *oyás'iŋ* "all": "my relatives, all". A form
   for "our relatives" exists (Riggs gives *uŋkitakuyepi*), and the phrase
   does not use it (archaeology/lakota/mitakuye-oyasin-form-and-sound.md
   §§1, 5).
   - (a) A widening "we": the top rung is the us widened to all beings. The
     "we" is then YOUSPEAK's reading, supplied by the English gloss "we are
     all related", not by the Lakota grammar.
   - (b) A widening standing-place: the top rung is the first rung widened,
     "I, among all my kin". *mitakuyame:* then stands nearer *mi:* than
     *yumi:*, and the ladder is one of standing-places, not of sizes of
     "we".
2. **mitakuyame: and the Lakota criticism of outsiders' use.** Wikipedia,
   citing the *Lakota Country Times*, records that the Ojibwe use of the
   phrase in a 1960s revival was criticised by some Lakota as the work of
   "cultural thieves". YOUSPEAK's use of the phrase as a liturgical anchor
   sits inside the same hazard. The Lakota dig says that review by Lakota
   speakers is desirable before the anchor is elaborated further, taught or
   set in a font, and that if Lakota speakers or a tribal body ask that the
   phrase not be used, or be used differently, the anchor is withdrawn or
   changed accordingly
   (archaeology/lakota/mitakuye-oyasin-form-and-sound.md §0). Candidate
   glyphs for U+E17F already exist, as candidates, not canon
   (script/glyphs/candidates/anchors-2026-11/).
   - (a) Keep *mitakuyame:* as it stands, under the dig's protocol.
   - (b) Hold it, registered but not taught or set in a font, until Lakota
     speakers have been asked whether YOUSPEAK should use the phrase at
     all.
   - (c) Take it out of anchor position. The Core word *mitakuyame* is a
     separate question.

   The same dig says that no respelling toward the attested *mitakuye* (its
   lead 1) should be made before Lakota speakers have been asked.
3. **"We, before You."** *yumi:* includes the one addressed by definition.
   A "we" that speaks to GoD without including the One addressed, as Tok
   Pisin prayer does with *mipela*, is exclusive, and the Inclusive
   Blessing gives the exclusive no anchor. Whether the chapter needs an
   anchored way to say "we, before You", and if so which:
   - (a) It needs none: such prayer is spoken in plain "we", unanchored,
     and the vocative O- carries the turn toward GoD.
   - (b) Each speaker stands from their own side, *mi:*, many first persons
     before You.
   - (c) The Inclusive Blessing is amended, or an anchor added, for a "we"
     addressed to the One outside it
     (archaeology/tok-pisin/pronouns-clusivity-and-number.md §8, lead 3).

   Whether `yumi: O-Theobasis, we receive Your kimme` stays as the chapter's
   example belongs to the same choice.
4. **A "we" wider than the two that still includes you.** With *yumi:* the
   dyad, an inclusive "we" of more than two (you and me and others, or me
   with several people addressed), which Tok Pisin *yumi* covers, has no
   anchor of its own in this chapter.
   - (a) It takes none and is spoken in plain "we", as the exclusive is.
   - (b) An anchor for it is sought through the forge.

## Appendix — projection-account (NOEMA / THE-REALM / GRASPING)

In realm-terms: a meaning grasped from one vantage is one projection of the
noema; the same meaning grasped from within the dyad is a **stereoscopic
projection** — two angles resolved into depth. The anchors are the grammar
of declaring which projection is being offered: *mi:* one eye, *yu:* the
other eye honored, *yumi:* the parallax that makes depth real. Ankyrance is
to persons what diplosemy is to meanings: the refusal to pretend there is
only one angle.

_Sixth chapter opened. The word now knows where it stands._
